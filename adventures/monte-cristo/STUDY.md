# STUDY NOTES — The Count of Monte Cristo (Dumas, 1844)

Source: Project Gutenberg #1184 (`book.txt` in this directory, 2.78 MB, 117
chapters). All quotes below were pulled from that file and verified against it;
line numbers refer to `book.txt` as downloaded 2026-08-09.

Reading strategy used: full close reads of ch. 1, 4-8 (Marseilles frame-up),
14-21 (Château d'If, escape), 23-24 (the island and the cave), 26-27
(Caderousse and the diamond), 30 (Morrel saved); targeted reads of the
Paris half for each revenge mechanism (ch. 60-61 telegraph, 63 Auteuil
dinner, 78/86 Janina, 82-83 Caderousse's death, 92 Morcerf's suicide,
108-111 Villefort's fall, 114-116 Danglars starved and pardoned, 117 ending).

## 1. TIMELINE OF BEATS

- **Feb 24, 1815** — the Pharaon makes Marseilles with her captain dead at
  sea; Dantès, 19, brought her home. Stopped at Elba per Captain Leclere's
  dying order; carries a letter for "M. Noirtier, Rue Coq-Héron, Paris."
- **Feb 27-28** — Danglars writes the denunciation at La Réserve with his
  left hand; Fernand posts it. Dantès arrested at his own betrothal feast
  (Feb 28, per Dantès: "Since the 28th of February, 1815").
- **Mar 1** — Villefort's examination. He is about to free Dantès until he
  reads the addressee: Noirtier is Villefort's own Bonapartist father.
  Villefort burns the letter and buries Dantès to bury the name.
- **1815-1821** — solitary in the Château d'If. Despair, near-starvation,
  then the scratching in the wall (year six).
- **~1821-1829** — Faria: the tunnel, the education (langs, math, history,
  chemistry), the deduction of the three enemies, the Spada treasure secret.
- **Feb 28, 1829 (night)** — Faria's third attack kills him. Dantès swaps
  himself into the burial sack; thrown from the rampart with a 36-pound
  shot roped to his feet. Cuts free underwater, swims a league through a
  rising mistral to the Île de Tiboulen. Storm; shipwreck; at dawn he hails
  a Genoese smuggler tartan (the Jeune-Amélie) in a drowned sailor's red cap.
- **~14 years total in If** ("You condemned me to a horrible, tedious death"
  ... "twenty-three years ago" is Villefort's marriage, 1815; the escape is
  1829 — fourteen years a prisoner, age 19 to 33).
- **1829** — smuggler rendezvous at Monte Cristo; feigned fall among the
  rocks buys him solitude; finds the Spada treasure. As Abbé Busoni, buys
  Caderousse's story with the diamond; recovers the red silk purse. As the
  English clerk of Thomson & French / Lord Wilmore, saves Morrel from
  suicide on **Sept 5, 1829** — receipted bill and a diamond marked
  "Julie's Dowry" in the red purse; the "new" Pharaon sails into harbor.
- **1838, Rome** — Sinbad the Sailor / the Count; carnival, catacombs of
  Saint Sebastian, Luigi Vampa's bandits; rescues Albert de Morcerf to buy
  his entrée into Paris.
- **1838-1839, Paris** — the machine runs: Auteuil dinner; the telegraph
  hack; Benedetto planted as "Prince Cavalcanti"; the Janina exposure and
  Morcerf's suicide; the Villefort house poisonings and the Assizes;
  Caderousse killed by Benedetto after burgling the Count's house;
  Danglars ruined, flees, is starved in Vampa's larder and pardoned.
- **Oct 5** — Morrel's son Maximilian given Valentine alive in the grotto;
  the letter: "Wait and hope." The white sail on the horizon.

## 2. LOCATION INVENTORY (with atmospheric quotes)

### Marseilles, 1815

**The harbor / the Pharaon.** "On the 24th of February, 1815, the look-out
at Notre-Dame de la Garde signalled the three-master, the _Pharaon_ from
Smyrna, Trieste, and Naples." The pilot boards "rounding the Château d'If"
— the prison is literally the first landmark of the book. Dantès on deck:
"a fine, tall, slim young fellow of eighteen or twenty, with black eyes,
and hair as dark as a raven's wing." Ship-handling texture for the opening
scene: "Stand by there to lower the topsails and jib; brail up the
spanker!" ... "Let go—and clue up!" ... "Half-mast the colors, and square
the yards!" (all ch. 1). Captain Leclere lies "sewn up in his hammock with
a thirty-six-pound shot at his head and his heels" — the same weight of
iron that will later be tied to Dantès's own feet. Use this echo.

**Morrel's presence** (the owner boards from a skiff): "Ah, is it you,
Dantès? ... why have you such an air of sadness aboard?" His verdict on
Danglars's sniping: "'You see,' said Danglars, 'he fancies himself captain
already.' — 'And so, in fact, he is,' said the owner."

**Allées de Meilhan** — old Dantès's room, fifth floor, No. 15. Later the
red purse is found "on the corner of a mantelpiece in a small room on the
fifth floor" (ch. 30) — the same mantel where Morrel left it.

**The Catalans** — Mercédès's fishing village on the point; from the
prison boat Dantès sees "One light alone was visible; and Dantès saw that
it came from Mercédès' chamber" (ch. 8).

**La Réserve** — the arbor of the seaside inn, scene of both the
conspiracy (ch. 4) and the wedding feast (ch. 5). The morning of the
feast: "The morning's sun rose clear and resplendent, touching the foamy
waves into a network of ruby-tinted light." The arrest: "Edmond Dantès,
I arrest you in the name of the law!" and the goodbye — "Adieu, adieu,
dearest Edmond!" / "Good-bye, Mercédès—we shall soon meet again!"
Passing the same windows that night in the police boat: "through the open
windows came the laughter and revelry of a ball."

**Villefort's study, Palais de Justice.** Villefort dangles freedom, then
reads the address. "'To Monsieur Noirtier, Rue Coq-Héron, Paris.' Had a
thunderbolt fallen into the room, Villefort could not have been more
stupefied." He burns the letter at the grate: "'You see, I destroy it?'
'Oh,' exclaimed Dantès, 'you are goodness itself.'" And alone: "This
accursed letter would have destroyed all my hopes... from this letter,
which might have ruined me, I will make my fortune."

### The Château d'If

Approach by night (ch. 8): "he saw rise within a hundred yards of him the
black and frowning rock on which stands the Château d'If. This gloomy
fortress... seemed to Dantès like a scaffold to a malefactor." The
garrison in one line: "There are only a governor, a garrison, turnkeys,
and good thick walls."

**Dantès's cell (No. 34).** Furniture inventory, verbatim gameable: "All
his furniture consisted of a bed, a chair, a table, a pail, and a jug."
The loophole: "furnished with three iron bars," narrowing to "an opening
through which a child could not have passed," looking on "a kind of open
gallery, where patrols are continually passing, and sentries keep watch
day and night." One wall on the corridor, one on solid rock, one adjoining
"the lower part of the governor's apartments."

**The tunnel.** Faria dug "about fifty feet" over years with a chisel made
from a bed-clamp, erring by an ellipse: "I took the wrong angle... I took
the wall you are mining for the outer wall of the fortress." His spoil
went down a hollowed staircase well "now so completely choked up."

**Faria's cell (No. 27).** Two hiding places: under the old hearth stone
(manuscript, pens, ink, lamp, tools) and behind the head of the bed (the
rope ladder, "between twenty-five and thirty feet in length").

**The burial ground.** The whole point, said plainly at the end of ch. 20:
"The sea is the cemetery of the Château d'If." The grave-diggers' joke:
"'Bad weather!' observed one of the bearers; 'not a pleasant night for a
dip in the sea.' 'Why, yes, the abbé runs a chance of being wet.'"

**Tiboulen.** "Before him rose a grotesque mass of rocks, that resembled
nothing so much as a vast fire petrified at the moment of its most fervent
combustion." Storm, the wrecked fishing boat, rainwater drunk from a rock
hollow, and at dawn the Genoese tartan out of Marseilles.

### Monte Cristo island

Ch. 23-24. Wild, uninhabited, granite: goats, lizards "glittering with the
hues of the emerald," grasshoppers, myrtle and olive scrub. The creek:
"hidden like the bath of some ancient nymph... deep in the centre, to
admit of the entrance of a small vessel." Notches cut in the rocks lead
from the creek to a circular rock resting on a man-made wedge. The dig:
olive-trunk lever fails; Jacopo's powder horn blows the wedge ("made a
match by rolling his handkerchief in saltpetre"); the rock rolls into the
sea, exposing "an iron ring let into a square flag-stone," then "steps
that descended... into the obscurity of a subterraneous grotto."

**First grotto**: "a dim and bluish light... granite that sparkled like
diamonds." Hidden second opening sealed with stones and painted stucco
("painted to imitate granite") found by sounding the wall — "one part of
the wall gave forth a more hollow and deeper echo."

**Second grotto**: "lower and more gloomy... At the left of the opening
was a dark and deep angle." Two feet of earth down: the oaken coffer,
Spada arms on a silver plate ("a sword, en pale, on an oval shield...
surmounted by a cardinal's hat"), lock burst with the pickaxe. Contents in
three compartments: "In the first, blazed piles of golden coin; in the
second, were ranged bars of unpolished gold... in the third, Edmond
grasped handfuls of diamonds, pearls, and rubies, which, as they fell on
one another, sounded like hail against glass." Count: a thousand ingots,
25,000 crowns, ten double handfuls of gems.

### The Pont du Gard Inn (Caderousse, 1829)

Ch. 26-27. A failing roadside inn between Bellegarde and Beaucaire.
Caderousse bolts the door to tell the story; his wife La Carconte listens
through the floorboards. Busoni trades the diamond ("it is worth fifty
thousand francs") for the story and for "the red silk purse that M. Morrel
left on old Dantès' chimney-piece." La Carconte's first words on seeing
the stone: "Suppose it's false?" — envy already at work.

### Rome (1838)

Sinbad's hashish den on Monte Cristo (ch. 31): "it is hashish—the purest
and most unadulterated hashish of Alexandria." The catacombs of Saint
Sebastian (ch. 37): Vampa's headquarters, where Albert is held and later
Danglars is starved (ch. 115: "Luigi Vampa's Bill of Fare" — a fowl costs
100,000 francs; "those who have died of hunger" suffered more than you).

### Paris (1838-39)

- **No. 30 Champs-Élysées** — the Count's house; the dressing-room where
  he keeps "a sailor's jacket and hat" (used for the Morcerf reveal).
- **The house at Auteuil** (Rue de la Fontaine 28) — bought deliberately;
  Bertuccio nearly faints touring it. The garden's plantain tree hides the
  infant box: "digging, found a box, or rather, the iron-work of a box, in
  the midst of which was the skeleton of a newly born infant" (staged
  declaration at the dinner, ch. 63; the skeleton is a bluff — Bertuccio
  had actually rescued the living child, Benedetto).
- **Danglars's bank / mansion, Rue de la Chaussée d'Antin** — unlimited
  credit on Thomson & French; the Count draws millions.
- **The telegraph tower** (ch. 60-61) — a semaphore relay manned by a poor
  gardener paid 1,000 francs a year, fined 100 for a missed signal. Monte
  Cristo: "Not even for fifteen years' wages?... there are fifteen of
  them" (bank-notes). The false signal fakes a Spanish revolution;
  Danglars dumps his Spanish bonds and loses a million.
- **The Morcerf house, Rue du Helder**; **the Chamber of Peers** where
  Haydée testifies; **Villefort's house, Faubourg Saint-Honoré** with
  Noirtier's ground-floor room and Valentine's bedroom; **the Assizes**
  where Benedetto names his father.

### The return to If (ch. 113)

The Count revisits his own cell and Faria's as a tourist while the
guide-book prison is shown off; recovers Faria's manuscript. This is the
novel's own epilogue geography — the story ends where it began.

## 3. CHARACTER ROSTER

### Edmond Dantès and his identities

- **The sailor (19)** — guileless, competent, beloved. His whole politics:
  "I love my father, I respect M. Morrel, and I adore Mercédès." Villefort
  reads him instantly: "every word the young man uttered convinced him
  more and more of his innocence."
- **Prisoner 34** — despair to iron purpose. At Faria's corpse: "Before I
  die I must not forget that I have my executioners to punish, and
  perhaps, too, who knows, some friends to reward." The swap resolution:
  "Since none but the dead pass freely from this dungeon, let me take the
  place of the dead!"
- **Abbé Busoni** — the confessor mask; used on Caderousse twice (the inn,
  the deathbed) and on Villefort at the end. Voice: measured, terrible
  mercy. "I come to tell you that you have sufficiently repaid your debt."
- **Lord Wilmore** — the eccentric English benefactor; Thomson & French's
  agent; the mask of anonymous generosity (Morrel rescue).
- **Sinbad the Sailor** — the oriental fantasist of Monte Cristo and Rome.
- **The Count** — "an adventurer sewn up in gold and jewellery" (Morcerf's
  sneer). Self-conception: "a man, who, like Satan, thought himself for an
  instant equal to God" (final letter). The doubt after Edouard's death:
  "he felt that he had passed beyond the bounds of vengeance, and that he
  could no longer say, 'God is for and with me.'"

### Abbé Faria (the game's warmest relationship)

Small, white-haired, black-bearded, eyes "deep-set, penetrating"; the mad
priest of If who is the sanest man in the book. Maker of things: chisel
from a bed-clamp, knife and penknife "out of an old iron candlestick,"
pens from whiting-head cartilage, ink from chimney soot in Sunday wine,
lamp oil from meat fat, fire from flint and burnt linen and scrounged
sulphur, paper from two shirts, a fish-bone needle, a 25-30 foot rope
ladder from raveled sheets re-hemmed so no one would notice. Teacher:
"when I have taught you mathematics, physics, history, and the three or
four modern languages... you will know as much as I do myself." "To learn
is not to know; there are the learners and the learned. Memory makes the
one, philosophy the other." Detective: "if you wish to discover the
author of any bad action, seek first to discover the person to whom the
perpetration of that bad action could be in any way advantageous" — then
walks Dantès step by step to Danglars (the overheard cabin conversation,
the left-handed writing: "that performed with the left hand is invariably
uniform"), Fernand ("he would more likely have got rid of me by sticking
a knife into me"), and, by inference on the examination, Villefort.
Warns his pupil what he has built: (paraphrase of ch. 17's close) he
regrets teaching Dantès the deduction, seeing hatred born. Dies of the
third cataleptic attack; his phial of red liquor (the emergency cordial)
fails him. Deathbed gift: "If we ever escape together, half this treasure
is yours; if I die here... it is yours alone."

### The four targets

- **Danglars (greed)** — supercargo, then baron and banker. "obsequious
  to his superiors, insolent to his subordinates." The author of the plot:
  "the French have the superiority over the Spaniards, that the Spaniards
  ruminate, while the French invent." Ends white-haired, pardoned,
  starved down to repentance in Vampa's catacombs.
- **Fernand / Count de Morcerf (betrayal)** — Catalan fisherman who mailed
  the letter, then deserted at Waterloo, spied in Spain, and "betrayed,
  sold, and murdered his benefactor, Ali" at Janina; bought his title with
  the proceeds of selling Ali's wife and daughter. Dies by his own pistol
  after the cry "Edmond Dantès!"
- **Villefort (ambition)** — deputy procureur who knew Dantès was innocent
  and buried him to protect his own name. "You condemned me to a horrible,
  tedious death; you killed my father; you deprived me of liberty, of
  love, and happiness." Ends mad among his dead.
- **Caderousse (envy)** — the drunk tailor neighbor who watched and said
  nothing ("I have always had more dread of a pen, a bottle of ink, and a
  sheet of paper, than of a sword or pistol" — his one flash of drunken
  clarity). Ruined by his own greed at every rung: the diamond (murders
  the jeweler), the galleys, blackmail, burglary; dies stabbed by
  Benedetto while Busoni watches: "I am neither the Abbé Busoni nor Lord
  Wilmore... think again,—do you not recollect me?"

### The others

- **Mercédès** — the only person who recognizes the Count unaided; begs
  for her son's life; leaves Fernand's house with nothing but Dantès's
  old small savings. "Courage, mother! Come, this is no longer our home!"
- **Haydée** — daughter of Ali Pasha of Janina, bought by the Count from
  slavery, his living evidence: "I am Haydée, the daughter of Ali
  Tepelini, pasha of Yanina, and of Vasiliki, his beloved wife." Carries
  the satin satchel with her birth record and the bill of her own sale
  signed by "Fernand Mondego." Loves the Count; he sails away with her.
- **M. Morrel** — the good owner who petitioned for Dantès through every
  regime and went to the brink of suicide with honor intact; saved at the
  literal last second ("He placed the muzzle of the pistol between his
  teeth. Suddenly he heard a cry—it was his daughter's voice"). His son
  **Maximilian** is the Count's redemption project in Paris.
- **Bertuccio** — Corsican steward; his confession (ch. 43-45) supplies
  the Auteuil evidence: he stabbed Villefort in that garden the night
  Villefort buried the box; the box held a living newborn (Benedetto)
  whom Bertuccio raised, to his sorrow.
- **Benedetto / "Andrea Cavalcanti"** — Villefort's bastard, forger,
  murderer, planted by the Count as a prince and engaged to Danglars's
  daughter; his arrest and courtroom parentage bomb ("The Assizes" /
  "The Indictment") detonates Villefort publicly.
- **Noirtier** — Villefort's Bonapartist father, paralyzed, communicates
  by eye-blinks; protects Valentine (builds her brucine tolerance);
  the original addressee of the fatal letter.
- **Luigi Vampa** — bandit chief, the Count's instrument in Rome.
- **Jacopo** — the smuggler who nearly dives after "drowning" Dantès at
  the island and is rewarded with a ship; loyalty's small mirror.

### The denunciation, verbatim (ch. 4)

> "The honorable, the king's attorney, is informed by a friend of the
> throne and religion, that one Edmond Dantès, mate of the ship
> _Pharaon_, arrived this morning from Smyrna, after having touched at
> Naples and Porto-Ferrajo, has been intrusted by Murat with a letter for
> the usurper, and by the usurper with a letter for the Bonapartist
> committee in Paris. Proof of this crime will be found on arresting him,
> for the letter will be found upon him, or at his father's, or in his
> cabin on board the _Pharaon_."

### The Spada parchment, assembled (ch. 18)

> "This 25th day of April, 1498, be...ing invited to dine by his Holiness
> Alexander VI., and fearing that not...content with making me pay for my
> hat, he may desire to become my heir, and re...serves for me the fate
> of Cardinals Caprara and Bentivoglio, who were poisoned,...I declare to
> my nephew, Guido Spada, my sole heir, that I have bu...ried in a place
> he knows and has visited with me, that is, in...the caves of the small
> Island of Monte Cristo, all I poss...essed of ingots, gold, money,
> jewels, diamonds, gems; that I alone...know of the existence of this
> treasure, which may amount to nearly two mil...lions of Roman crowns,
> and which he will find on raising the twentieth ro...ck from the small
> creek to the east in a right line. Two open...ings have been made in
> these caves; the treasure is in the furthest a...ngle in the second;
> which treasure I bequeath and leave en...tire to him as my sole heir.
> '25th April, 1498. Cæs...ar † Spada.'"

The right-edge fragment survived (Faria found it in a forgotten breviary,
nearly burned it, saw letters appear in the flame); the left side is
Faria's deduction — a built-in two-piece puzzle: the player reads a
useless half, then must get the other from Faria.

## 5. THE ESCAPE, MECHANICALLY (game centerpiece)

Sequence as written, each step gameable:

1. **The sound.** Recovering from his hunger strike, Dantès hears "a kind
   of scratching" in the wall. Test protocol he invents himself: knock
   three times with a detached stone — "At the first blow the sound
   ceased, as if by magic." Silence for three days = it's a prisoner, not
   a workman (a workman sanctioned by the governor would have resumed).
2. **First tool.** No knife; grating solid; bed clamps screwed down; pail
   handle removed. "Dantès had but one resource, which was to break the
   jug, and with one of the sharp fragments attack the wall." Cover
   story: it fell while he drank. Jailer grumbles, fetches another.
3. **Method discovery.** Night work fails on the stone; day reveals he
   should attack the friable plaster around it, not the stone. Yield math
   Dumas supplies: "in two years... a passage twenty feet long and two
   feet broad, might be formed."
4. **The lever.** Nails too weak, shards break on the hewn stone. The
   trick: put the plate by the door; the jailer steps on it and breaks
   it; there is nothing to pour soup into, so "Leave the saucepan." Next
   day the annoyed jailer makes it permanent. The straightened iron
   handle levers the stone out in an hour — "a cavity a foot and a half
   in diameter." Plaster hidden under earth in the corner; stone
   replaced by day; bed pushed back.
5. **The obstacle & the voice.** A ceiling beam blocks the hole. Despair
   prayer — answered: "Who talks of God and despair at the same time?"
   The catechism through the wall (name, country, crime, date), Faria's
   crushing realization he has surfaced 15 feet short inside another
   cell, and "It is well... tomorrow."
6. **Breakthrough.** The floor gives way; Faria climbs up into cell 34.
   Reconnaissance: the human-pyramid at the loophole (Dantès braces
   against the wall; Faria stands on his shoulders) reveals sentries on
   the open gallery — no exit that way. Escape shelved; education begins.
7. **The education (years compressed).** Languages, math, physics,
   history; and the deduction that gives the revenge its target list.
   Faria's two years estimate; "At the end of a year Dantès was a new
   man." (Also: Faria digs a passage under the gallery in a later plan;
   the second attack paralyzes his arm and leg and ends it.)
8. **The treasure secret** after the second attack: the story of Cæsar
   Spada, the breviary, the parchment; "If we ever escape together, half
   this treasure is yours; if I die here... it is yours alone."
9. **Faria's death.** Third attack; the cordial fails ("only now the fit
   is stronger"). Doctor certifies death; the red-hot iron to the heel —
   "this burn in the heel is decisive" — Dantès smells the burning flesh
   through the wall. Sack sewn; burial set for "about ten or eleven
   o'clock" this evening; no chaplain (away at Hyères) so no vigil:
   "Shut the dungeon as if he were alive—that is all."
10. **The swap.** Dantès's own idea, the moment the game must let the
    player have: "Since none but the dead pass freely from this dungeon,
    let me take the place of the dead!" Steps as written: open the sack
    with Faria's knife; carry the corpse through the tunnel to his own
    bed; tie the corpse's head with his own night-rag, turn its face to
    the wall, cover it; return; take needle and thread; strip; enter the
    sack; "sewed up the mouth of the sack from the inside." Contingency
    plans he rehearses: knife ready to rip out if discovered on the
    bier; if buried, dig up through loose earth at night. The dread
    foreshadow he cannot parse: "'Have you tied the knot?'... 'What's
    the knot for?' thought Dantès."
11. **The drop.** Carried out; mistral on his face; a pause while a
    grave-digger fetches "a heavy metallic substance" — the 36-pound
    shot — corded to his feet. Up steps, swung — "One!... two! three!"
    — flung, "dragged into its depths by a thirty-six-pound shot tied to
    his feet. The sea is the cemetery of the Château d'If."
12. **Underwater.** Knife already open in his right hand ("prepared as he
    was for every chance"): rip the sack, free arm and body — the shot
    still drags him by the feet — bend double, cut the cord "at the
    moment when it seemed as if he were actually strangled," surface,
    breathe once, dive again to avoid the torch watching from the
    rampart.
13. **The swim.** Ratonneau, Pomègue, Daume are inhabited; Tiboulen and
    Lemaire safe, "a league from the Château d'If." Navigation: "he saw
    the light of Planier... By leaving this light on the right, he kept
    the Island of Tiboulen a little on the left." An hour-plus in rising
    seas; touches rock with a knee-scrape at the moment of despair.
14. **Tiboulen.** Sleeps on granite; woken by thunder; shelters under an
    overhanging rock; drinks rainwater from a hollow; watches a fishing
    boat wreck with all hands. Dawn inventory of doom: the body will be
    found, the tunnel discovered, the cannon will warn the coast, "I am
    cold, I am hungry. I have lost even the knife that saved me."
15. **The tartan.** A Genoese smuggler out of Marseilles; he swims out
    (on a wreck-spar), sells himself as a shipwrecked Maltese sailor —
    the red cap from a drowned sailor completes the costume — and is
    taken aboard the Jeune-Amélie. The If cannon fires behind him;
    nobody aboard cares. Free.

## 6. THE FOUR REVENGES, MECHANICALLY

### Caderousse (envy) — "the diamond trap"
Instrument: his own greed, fed twice by the Count. (1) 1829: Busoni pays
the diamond for testimony; Caderousse and La Carconte murder the jeweler
who buys it (told in Bertuccio's confession) — galleys. (2) 1838-39:
escaped, blackmailing Benedetto, he burgles the Count's house on a tip
the Count himself arranged (the Count, tipped by Benedetto's anonymous
note, receives him as Busoni, watches him try, makes him sign a letter
exposing "Andrea Cavalcanti," lets him leave — Benedetto knifes him at
the gate). Deathbed: three drops of cordial keep him alive long enough
for the identity ladder — Busoni? Wilmore? — "I am Edmond Dantès" (said
by unmasking: wig off, black hair falls). Caderousse dies with the
count's terrible arithmetic of Providence read over him.

### Morcerf/Fernand (betrayal) — "the public exposure"
Instruments: journalism + testimony + the Chamber of Peers. Steps: the
Count has Danglars write to his Janina correspondent; Beauchamp's paper
prints the first squib ("We hear from Yanina"); the inquiry convenes the
same evening Morcerf thinks he can bluff it; Haydée appears with the
documents — birth record, baptism record, and the bill of her own sale
signed "Fernand Mondego" — and the committee votes his disgrace. Albert
challenges the Count; Mercédès begs at midnight and is the one person
told the whole truth; Albert publicly apologizes on the field instead of
firing. Fernand storms to the Count demanding his real name; the Count
changes into the sailor's jacket and hat: "a face you must often have
seen in your dreams since your marriage with Mercédès, my betrothed!"
Fernand staggers home in time to see wife and son leave forever, and
shoots himself. The reveal word is a single cry: "Edmond Dantès!"

### Danglars (greed) — "the engineered bankruptcy"
Death by a thousand ledger cuts, all traceable in the text: (1) the
letter of unlimited credit on Thomson & French — the Count draws and
draws (6M across the book); (2) the telegraph hack — 25,000 francs to a
semaphore gardener transmits a false Spanish coup; Danglars dumps his
Spanish bonds, "a million" lost overnight; (3) the fake prince
Cavalcanti engaged to his daughter — social capital vaporized at the
arrest; (4) matched trading losses fed by planted news until five
million of deposits (hospital trust funds) are all that's left; Danglars
draws it as a receipt on Rome and runs. (5) In Rome, Peppino (whose life
the Count bought) delivers him to Vampa; the catacombs larder sells
dinner at 100,000 francs a fowl until the 5,000,050 francs are eaten
down to 50,000 and the miser begs: "Take my last gold... I only ask to
live!" The cloaked figure asks, "Do you repent?" — pardon, freedom, and
hair gone white at a stream. The restitution: "The 5,000,000 you stole
from the hospitals has been restored to them by an unknown hand."

### Villefort (ambition) — "the house of poison"
Two convergent trains, both merely *permitted* by the Count: (1) the
Auteuil secret — the Count buys the murder house, stages the dinner
declaration over the dug-up infant box while watching Villefort and Mme
Danglars stiffen ("is it not a crime to bury a living child in a
garden?"); Bertuccio's confession supplies the true history (Villefort
buried his bastard alive; Bertuccio stabbed him and unknowingly saved
the child, Benedetto). (2) The poisoner in Villefort's own house — Mme
de Villefort, armed with the Count's toxicology seminar (ch. 52),
murders the Saint-Mérans and Barrois and nearly Valentine (saved by
Noirtier's brucine tolerance-building, then by the Count's staged death
and rescue). Villefort, prosecuting Benedetto at the Assizes, is named
in open court as the prisoner's father; goes home, forces his wife to
account — she poisons herself and Edouard; Busoni is waiting in
Noirtier's room. Unmasking ladder again: "It is the face of the Count of
Monte Cristo!" — "You must go farther back." — "I am Edmond Dantès!"
Villefort drags him to the bodies: "There, Edmond Dantès! ... see, are
you well avenged?" — and goes mad. This is where the revenge curdles;
the Count "felt that he had passed beyond the bounds of vengeance."

## 8. ADAPTATION NOTES (what the game keeps, cuts, compresses)

- **Keep whole:** Acts I-III are already an adventure game — Dumas wrote
  object puzzles (jug, plate, saucepan, needle, parchment, powder keg)
  with solutions and failure states. Use his mechanics nearly verbatim.
- **Compress:** the six despair years and the education years become
  narrated time-skips between playable set pieces (the engine can print
  "Months pass." between turns); Rome shrinks to the catacombs finale of
  the Danglars quest; the Morrel rescue becomes the emotional finale of
  Act III (purse on the mantel) rather than a spread of chapters.
- **Cut:** Albert/Franz in Rome, the Beauchamp journalism duel,
  Valentine/Maximilian courtship (kept only as the rescue and ending),
  Eugénie, Debray, the Saint-Méran deaths (referenced, not played),
  Lucien/opera society scenes, Benedetto's backstory beyond what the
  Auteuil quest needs.
- **Reorder for play:** Caderousse's inn scene sits naturally on the road
  in Act III (post-island), which also front-loads the "what happened
  while I was buried" exposition exactly as the novel does (Caderousse
  narrates father's death, Mercédès's marriage, Morrel's ruin).
- **The moral arc** must survive compression: Edouard's death (or a
  softened equivalent) checks the player's rampage before the pardon of
  Danglars and the gift to Morrel — wrath, then doubt, then mercy, then
  "wait and hope."
