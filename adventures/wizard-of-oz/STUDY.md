# STUDY NOTES — The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (L. Frank Baum, 1900)

Source: `book.txt` (Project Gutenberg #55, verified full Baum text, 24 chapters,
Introduction dated "Chicago, April, 1900"). Line numbers below refer to book.txt.

**Rights note:** the 1900 book is public domain. The 1939 MGM film is NOT.
Everything in these notes is from the book. Film divergences are flagged
inline as **FILM TRAP** so the design never drifts toward protected material.

## 1. Location inventory (in narrative order)

1. **Kansas farmhouse** (Ch I, lines 110-222). One room: rusty cookstove,
   cupboard, table, three or four chairs, big bed and little bed, trap door
   in the middle of the floor with a ladder down to the cyclone cellar.
   Everything gray: "Not a tree nor a house broke the broad sweep of flat
   country that reached to the edge of the sky in all directions." Uncle
   Henry runs for the stock; Aunt Em goes down the cellar; Toto hides under
   the bed, Dorothy catches him, misses the cellar, house rises "as easily as
   you could carry a feather." Toto falls through the open trap door and is
   held up by air pressure — Dorothy drags him back by the ear and closes it.
   She falls asleep in the flying house.

2. **Munchkin country, landing site** (Ch II, 231-463). "Lovely patches of
   greensward... stately trees bearing rich and luscious fruits," a small
   brook. The house corner rests on the Wicked Witch of the East: "two feet
   were sticking out, shod in silver shoes with pointed toes." The feet dry
   up in the sun and vanish, leaving only the shoes. Cast: three Munchkin
   men (blue, pointed hats with bells) and the **Witch of the North** (white
   hat and gown with little stars, wrinkled, walks stiffly). Her cap becomes
   a slate: "LET DOROTHY GO TO THE CITY OF EMERALDS." She kisses Dorothy's
   forehead: "no one will dare injure a person who has been kissed by the
   Witch of the North" — a round, shining mark. She exits by whirling three
   times on her left heel. Oz geography: deadly desert on all four sides;
   Munchkins East (blue), Quadlings South (red), Winkies West (yellow, under
   the Wicked Witch of the West), Gillikins North implied; Emerald City in
   the exact center.

3. **The road of yellow brick, Munchkin stretch** (Ch III, 516-601). Blue
   fences, round domed blue houses, farms. Dorothy preps in the house first:
   bread from the cupboard into a basket with a white cloth, gingham dress,
   pink sunbonnet, and — seeing her old shoes are worn — the silver shoes,
   which "fitted her as well as if they had been made for her" and "could
   not wear out." She locks the house door and pockets the key. Overnight
   stop at rich Munchkin **Boq's** house (dancing, five fiddlers). Boq:
   blue is Munchkin color, white is the witch color, so her blue-and-white
   check frock marks her a friendly witch.

4. **The Scarecrow's cornfield** (Ch III, 596-700). Beyond a blue fence off
   the road. Scarecrow on a pole: sack head stuffed with straw, painted
   face, old pointed blue hat, faded blue suit, blue-topped boots. He winks.
   "If you will please take away the pole I shall be greatly obliged to
   you." Dorothy lifts him off (he's light). His secret fear: "a lighted
   match."

5. **Rough road and the great forest** (Ch IV, 707-867). Broken bricks,
   holes; the brainless Scarecrow walks straight into them and falls,
   laughing. Trees meet over the road, darkness. A log cottage with a bed
   of dried leaves where Dorothy sleeps.

6. **The Tin Woodman's forest** (Ch V, 876-1095). A spring of clear water;
   a groan; a partly chopped tree and a man of tin frozen "with an uplifted
   axe in his hands," rusted for a year. "Get an oil-can and oil my joints...
   You will find an oil-can on a shelf in my cottage." Order: neck first,
   then arms (Scarecrow bends the joints free), then legs. He asks Dorothy
   to carry the oil-can in the basket in case of rain. First service: chops
   through a blockade where "trees and branches grew so thick over the road
   that the travelers could not pass."

7. **Deep forest, the Lion's ambush** (Ch VI, 1104-1281). A terrible roar;
   the Lion bowls the Scarecrow over, dents his claws on the Woodman, and
   opens his mouth to bite Toto — Dorothy slaps his nose: "Don't you dare to
   bite Toto!" ... "You are nothing but a big coward." "I know it," said the
   Lion, hanging his head in shame. Toto is "a meat dog." The beetle
   incident: the Woodman steps on a beetle, weeps, his jaws rust shut; the
   Scarecrow oils them. "Crying rusts my jaws so that I cannot speak."

8. **The first gorge** (Ch VII, 1322-1375). A wide, deep ditch across the
   road, jagged rocks below, unclimbable sides. The Lion: "I think I could
   jump over it." Scarecrow rides first ("the fall would not hurt me at
   all"), then Dorothy with Toto, then the Woodman. "Why don't you run and
   jump?" — "Because that isn't the way we Lions do these things."

9. **The Kalidah gorge** (Ch VII, 1377-1453). Kalidahs: "monstrous beasts
   with bodies like bears and heads like tigers," claws that could tear the
   Lion in two. A second gulf too broad to leap. Scarecrow's plan: the
   Woodman chops a tree by the ditch, the Lion pushes it over for a bridge.
   Two Kalidahs charge; the Lion's roar checks them; they cross the tree
   bridge behind the party; at the Scarecrow's word the Woodman chops away
   the near end and the Kalidahs are "dashed to pieces on the sharp rocks."

10. **The broad river** (Ch VII-VIII, 1455-1631). Woodman builds a raft of
    logs pinned with wooden pegs (takes overnight). Lion in the middle tips
    it; Scarecrow and Woodman pole from the ends. Midstream the current
    sweeps them off course toward "the country of the Wicked Witch of the
    West"; the Scarecrow poles too hard, the pole sticks in the mud, and the
    raft leaves him "clinging to the pole in the middle of the river." The
    Lion swims towing the raft by his tail. Walking back upriver they find
    the Scarecrow; a **Stork** carries him ashore ("He isn't heavy a bit...
    he is stuffed with straw").

11. **The Deadly Poppy Field** (Ch VIII, 1633-1707). "When there are many of
    these flowers together their odor is so powerful that anyone who
    breathes it falls asleep, and if the sleeper is not carried away from
    the scent of the flowers, he sleeps on and on forever." Dorothy and Toto
    fall asleep; straw and tin are immune. The Lion is told to run; he falls
    "only a short distance from the end of the poppy bed." Scarecrow and
    Woodman carry Dorothy out chair-of-hands style; the Lion is "much too
    heavy to lift."

12. **The Queen of the Field Mice** (Ch IX, 1716-1876). A yellow Wildcat
    chases a little gray field mouse; the Woodman beheads the Wildcat with
    one blow. The mouse is the Queen of all the Field Mice: "hereafter you
    must all serve him, and obey his slightest wish." The debt repaid:
    thousands of mice with strings haul the Lion out of the poppies on a
    wooden truck the Woodman builds from tree limbs. "If ever you need us
    again, come out into the field and call" — Ch XIV confirms Dorothy
    carries "the little whistle... the Queen of the Mice had given it to
    her" (the gift itself happens off-page; the design can stage it).

13. **The green country and the farmhouse** (Ch X, 1904-2032). Green fences,
    green houses, people afraid of the Lion. Farmhouse supper (porridge,
    scrambled eggs, white bread); the man with the hurt leg delivers the Oz
    rumor package: nobody sees Oz; he appears as a bird, an elephant, a cat,
    a beautiful fairy, a brownie, "But who the real Oz is, when he is in his
    own form, no living person can tell." Also the comic wish-list echoes
    (brains/heart/courage/Kansas) and "But what do YOU want?" to Toto.

14. **The gate of the Emerald City** (Ch X, 2040-2106). Green wall, high and
    thick; a gate studded with emeralds; a bell button that rings "a silvery
    tinkle." Inside, a high arched room and the **Guardian of the Gates**
    (green clothes, greenish skin, big green box). The green spectacles:
    "if you did not wear spectacles the brightness and glory of the Emerald
    City would blind you. Even those who live in the City must wear
    spectacles night and day. They are all locked on... and I have the only
    key." Locked with two golden bands behind the head; even Toto gets a
    pair. A big golden key from a peg opens the inner gate.

15. **The Emerald City** (Ch XI, 2115-2213). Green marble houses studded
    with emeralds, green glass panes, green sky. Green candy, green
    pop-corn, green shoes, green lemonade bought with green pennies. No
    horses; men push little green carts. The Palace in the exact middle:
    **soldier with the green whiskers** at the door; the green mat to wipe
    feet; the **green girl** maid; Dorothy's room with a green perfume
    fountain, green books with funny pictures, wardrobe of green dresses.
    NPC comedy: Scarecrow stands all night staring at a spider; Woodman
    moves his joints up and down all night; Lion curls up like a cat.

16. **The Throne Room — four audiences** (Ch XI, 2215-2529). One visitor per
    day, alone. The big round room, arched roof, great light like the sun.
    - **Dorothy sees an enormous Head** — bald, no body — eyes move, mouth
      moves: "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible. Who are you, and why do you
      seek me?" / "I am Dorothy, the Small and Meek." He interrogates the
      silver shoes and the kiss mark. His bargain: "In this country everyone
      must pay for everything he gets... Kill the Wicked Witch of the West."
    - **Scarecrow sees a lovely Lady** — green silk gauze, jeweled crown,
      fluttering wings. Same demand: "I don't care who kills her."
    - **Woodman sees a terrible Beast** — big as an elephant, rhinoceros
      head with five eyes, five arms, five legs, woolly hair.
    - **Lion sees a Ball of Fire** — so hot it singes his whiskers. "Bring
      me proof that the Wicked Witch is dead."
    Departure prep: axe sharpened on a green grindstone, fresh straw and new
    eye paint for the Scarecrow, basket refilled, a little bell for Toto.

17. **The Winkie country** (Ch XII, 2537-2712). No road west: "Keep to the
    West, where the sun sets." Away from the city the green silk dress turns
    pure white. Rough, hilly, untilled, treeless land. The Witch's escalating
    attacks (see quest logic below): forty wolves, forty crows, black bees,
    a dozen Winkie spearmen, then the Winged Monkeys.

18. **The Witch's castle** (Ch XII, 2759-2929). Yellow castle; the Lion
    penned in "a small yard with a high iron fence"; the kitchen where
    Dorothy must "clean the pots and kettles and sweep the floor and keep
    the fire fed with wood"; the cupboard Dorothy raids at night to feed the
    Lion; the Golden Cap in the Witch's cupboard; the bucket of water that
    stood near; beautiful rooms glimpsed in passing.

19. **The rescue country** (Ch XIII, 2938-3066). The rocky plain where the
    battered Woodman lies (axe rusted, handle broken); the tall smooth tree
    holding the bundle of the Scarecrow's clothes; the Yellow Castle rooms
    where Winkie tinsmiths work "three days and four nights." Parting gifts:
    golden collars for Toto and the Lion, a diamond bracelet for Dorothy, a
    gold-headed walking stick for the Scarecrow, a jeweled silver oil-can
    for the Woodman.

20. **Lost in the yellow fields** (Ch XIV, 3075-3167). No road back; endless
    "great fields of buttercups and yellow daisies." Dorothy blows the mouse
    whistle; the Queen points out the charm "written inside the Golden Cap."
    First Cap use: monkeys carry everyone to the Emerald City gate. In
    flight, the Monkey King tells the Gayelette/Quelala backstory.

21. **Emerald City again — the humbug** (Ch XV-XVI, 3288-3756). Oz stalls
    for days until the Scarecrow threatens to call the Winged Monkeys (Oz
    "had once met the Winged Monkeys... and he did not wish to meet them
    again"); appointment at "four minutes after nine o'clock." Empty throne
    room, a disembodied Voice ("I am everywhere"). The Lion roars, Toto
    jumps in alarm and "tipped over the screen that stood in a corner" — a
    little old bald wrinkled man. "I have been making believe." / "You're a
    humbug." / "Exactly so!" The props room: papier-mache Head on a wire
    with a thread for eyes and mouth; the Lady's mask and dress; the Beast
    is skins sewn round slats; the Ball of Fire is a cotton ball that burns
    when oiled. Backstory: born in Omaha, ventriloquist, circus balloonist,
    ropes twisted, landed here, played wizard, ordered the city built, put
    green spectacles on everyone ("when you wear green spectacles, why of
    course everything you see looks green to you").

22. **The gifts** (Ch XVI, 3628-3756). Brains: head unfastened, filled with
    "bran mixed with a great many pins and needles" — "bran-new brains,"
    "That is proof that he is sharp." Heart: square hole tinned open, "a
    pretty heart, made entirely of silk and stuffed with sawdust." Courage:
    a green bottle poured into a green-gold dish — "courage is always inside
    one; so that this really cannot be called courage until you have
    swallowed it."

23. **The balloon** (Ch XVII, 3765-3894). Green silk strips sewn three days,
    painted inside with glue, a big clothes basket on ropes. Cover story: a
    visit "to a great brother Wizard who lived in the clouds"; "While I am
    gone the Scarecrow will rule over you." Toto runs into the crowd after a
    kitten; Dorothy retrieves him; "crack! went the ropes" — the balloon
    rises without her. "I can't come back, my dear. Good-bye!"

24. **Away to the South** (Ch XVIII, 3903-4052). Second Cap use WASTED: "I
    want you to fly with me to Kansas" — "That cannot be done... We belong
    to this country alone." The soldier suggests **Glinda**, Witch of the
    South, most powerful of all, castle on the desert's edge; the road south
    is "full of dangers... wild beasts... a race of queer men who do not
    like strangers."

25. **The Fighting Trees** (Ch XIX, 4121-4165). The first row of a thick
    wood bends branches down, seizes the Scarecrow and flings him back
    twice. The Woodman chops a big branch in two; "the tree began shaking
    all its branches as if in pain" and the party passes; Toto is grabbed by
    a small branch (chopped off too). "These were the policemen of the
    forest."

26. **The Dainty China Country** (Ch XIX-XX, 4166-4369). A high smooth white
    china wall; the Woodman builds a wooden ladder; everyone says "Oh, my!"
    at the top. A country with a floor "as smooth and shining and white as
    the bottom of a big platter"; china houses waist-high; china milkmaids,
    princesses, shepherds, clowns, none higher than Dorothy's knee. Getting
    down: the Scarecrow falls off the wall and the others jump onto him as a
    cushion. Incidents: the frightened cow kicks — broken cow leg, shattered
    pail, milkmaid's nicked elbow ("I must take her to the mender's shop");
    the china Princess — "if I run I may fall down and break myself" and
    "one is never so pretty after being mended"; **Mr. Joker**, mended in a
    hundred places, recites: "My lady fair, / Why do you stare / At poor old
    Mr. Joker? ..." The Princess declines Dorothy's mantel invitation: taken
    away, china people stiffen. Exit over a lower wall standing on the
    Lion's back; his leap smashes a china church with his tail.

27. **The Lion's forest** (Ch XXI, 4378-4479). Bogs and marsh, then a grand
    old forest. Hundreds of beasts in assembly: their enemy is "a most
    tremendous monster, like a great spider," body big as an elephant, legs
    long as tree trunks, eight of them, teeth a foot long — but a neck "as
    slender as a wasp's waist." The Lion attacks it asleep, springs on its
    back, one blow knocks its head off. The beasts bow to their King; he
    promises to return once Dorothy is safely away.

28. **The Hammer-Heads' hill** (Ch XXII, 4488-4558). A steep rocky hill.
    "Keep back!... This hill belongs to us." Armless men, short and stout,
    flat-topped heads on telescoping wrinkled necks that shoot out like
    battering rams. The Scarecrow, then the Lion, get knocked rolling
    downhill. "It is useless to fight people with shooting heads; no one
    can withstand them." Third Cap use: carried over the hill.

29. **The Quadling country and Glinda's castle** (Ch XXII-XXIII, 4566-4741).
    Red fences, chubby good-natured Quadlings, a farmhouse dinner with
    "three kinds of cake and four kinds of cookies, and a bowl of milk for
    Toto." Three girl soldiers in red-and-gold at the castle gate. Glinda:
    ruby throne, rich red hair, white dress, blue eyes, young. Her price:
    "you must give me the Golden Cap." Her three planned uses: carry the
    Scarecrow to the Emerald City, the Woodman to the Winkies, the Lion to
    his forest — then the Cap goes to the Monkey King "that he and his band
    may thereafter be free for evermore."

30. **Home** (Ch XXIII-XXIV, 4696-4779). The shoe secret: "they can carry
    you to any place in the world in three steps... All you have to do is to
    knock the heels together three times and command the shoes to carry you
    wherever you wish to go." Dorothy: "Take me home to Aunt Em!" Three
    steps, whirling air; the Silver Shoes fall off mid-flight, "lost forever
    in the desert." She lands by the NEW farmhouse Uncle Henry built. Aunt
    Em watering cabbages: "My darling child!" Last line: "I'm so glad to be
    at home again!"

## 2. Character roster — voices and reusable dialogue

**Dorothy** — plainspoken, polite, brave when someone she loves is
threatened, otherwise practical. "I am Dorothy, the Small and Meek." "There
is no place like home" (yes, this exact line is in the BOOK, Ch IV line 745,
as table talk — not an incantation; see FILM TRAP below). "He's a—a—a meat
dog." When wronged she acts first and apologizes after (slaps the Lion,
throws the bucket, then "I'm very sorry, indeed").

**Toto** — never speaks ("But what do YOU want?" ... Toto only wagged his
tail; for, strange to say, he could not speak). Behavior engine: hides under
beds, barks at birds/kittens/witches-once-gone, chases mice and butterflies,
bites the Witch's leg (she doesn't bleed — "the blood in her had dried up
many years before"), and tips over the screen — Toto exposes the Wizard.

**The Scarecrow** — the planner; every big puzzle solution in the book is
his idea (ride the Lion, chop the tree bridge, cut the bridge behind them,
build a raft, mice + truck, call the Monkeys, ask the soldier). Voice:
cheerfully self-deprecating logic. "If this road goes in, it must come out."
"Anyone would know that." / "Certainly; that is why I know it. If it
required brains to figure it out, I never should have said it." Fears: "a
lighted match." Immune to: falls, water (soggy but fine), poppies, bees.
Weak: fire, being unstuffed, clumsy padded hands.

**The Tin Woodman** — the builder and the blade; courtly, tender-hearted
precisely because he thinks he has no heart. "I have no heart, you know, so
I am careful to help all those who may need a friend, even if it happens to
be only a mouse." Weeps at any harm done (the beetle) and RUSTS — jaws
first. Backstory: the enchanted axe took his limbs one by one, then his
head, then split his body; the tinsmith rebuilt everything; the lost
Munchkin maiden still waits. Immune to: claws, stings, poppies. Weak: rain,
tears, dents; needs the oil-can. Comic register: "There were forty wolves,
and forty times a wolf was killed" delivered as politely as a weather
report; "It was a good fight, friend."

**The Cowardly Lion** — the muscle who narrates his own terror while doing
the brave thing anyway. "Whenever there is danger, my heart begins to beat
fast." / "Perhaps you have heart disease," said the Tin Woodman. Wipes tears
with the tip of his tail. Leaps gorges (with riders), swims towing the raft,
roars armies away, beheads the great spider. Weak: poppies (flesh), fear of
Kalidahs, fire.

**Oz the Wizard** — four forms, then the fifth and true one: "a little old
man, with a bald head and a wrinkled face." Catchphrase in every form: "I am
Oz, the Great and Terrible. Who are you, and why do you seek me?" Post-reveal
voice: sheepish showman. "I'm just a common man." / "You're more than that...
you're a humbug." / "Exactly so! I am a humbug." "I'm really a very good
man, but I'm a very bad Wizard." His gift speeches are genuinely wise
(experience brings knowledge; "True courage is facing danger when you are
afraid"; hearts make most people unhappy) — then he hands over props anyway.

**The Wicked Witch of the West** — one eye "as powerful as a telescope."
Carries an umbrella (not a broom). Commands via a silver whistle: one blast
wolves, two crows, three bees. Owns the Golden Cap (two uses spent:
enslaving the Winkies; driving Oz out of the West). Afraid of the DARK, and
more afraid of WATER — "the old Witch never touched water, nor ever let
water touch her in any way." Cannot touch Dorothy because of the kiss; wants
the shoes; sets the invisible iron bar trip-trap; melts "like brown sugar"
with the great exit line: "I have been wicked in my day, but I never thought
a little girl like you would ever be able to melt me and end my wicked
deeds. Look out—here I go!"

**The Witch of the North** — sweet, elderly, modest ("I am not as powerful
as the Wicked Witch was... or I should have set the people free myself").
Gives the kiss and the slate prophecy. Counts "One, two, three" with her cap
balanced on her nose.

**Glinda** — young, beautiful, red-haired, on a ruby throne; kind but a
dealmaker (the Cap for the secret) who spends her three wishes ferrying
Dorothy's friends home and then frees the Monkeys. "You are unusual," she
tells the Scarecrow, deadpan.

**The Guardian of the Gates** — fussy, kindly gatekeeper; runs the
spectacles ritual both ways. "What! are you back again?... But I thought you
had gone to visit the Wicked Witch of the West." / "She could not help it,
for she is melted."

**The soldier with the green whiskers** — never sees Oz himself; relays
messages; timidly helpful; supplies the Glinda lead.

**The Queen of the Field Mice** — tiny, dignified, indignant ("Only a
mouse! Why, I am a Queen!"), commands thousands, repays debts, warns that
the Monkeys "think it great fun to plague us."

**Boq**, the green girl, the farmhouse man with the hurt leg, the Stork
("I always like to help anyone in trouble"), the china Princess and Mr.
Joker, the Monkey King (courtly, bound by the Cap, tells the Gayelette
story) — all one-scene NPCs with distinct voices.

## 4. Quest logic, as the book states it

- **What each companion wants:** Scarecrow, brains ("It is such an
  uncomfortable feeling to know one is a fool"); Tin Woodman, a heart ("no
  one can love who has not a heart"; he means to go back and marry the
  Munchkin girl); Lion, courage ("my life is simply unbearable without a bit
  of courage"); Dorothy, Kansas.
- **The Wizard's price**, stated four times in four shapes: kill (or help
  kill, or bring proof of the death of) the Wicked Witch of the West. "In
  this country everyone must pay for everything he gets."
- **How the Witch actually dies:** she cannot hurt Dorothy (kiss) and fears
  the shoes; she enslaves Dorothy instead, then trips her with the invisible
  iron bar and steals one silver shoe. Dorothy, angry for the first time,
  dashes the standing bucket of water over her. Water was always the answer;
  the Witch's own theft provokes it. Dorothy did not know ("Of course not...
  How should I?").
- **The Golden Cap's transfer chain:** Gayelette/Quelala → (eventually) the
  Wicked Witch (2 uses spent) → Dorothy (3 fresh uses: Emerald City; the
  refused Kansas flight; the Hammer-Heads) → Glinda (3 uses: sending the
  three friends to their kingdoms) → the Monkey King, freeing the band.
  Ownership = wearing/holding it; each new owner gets exactly three.
- **How the shoes actually work:** knock the heels together three times and
  command; three giant steps, each in the wink of an eye. **FILM TRAP:**
  the film's "There's no place like home" incantation-while-clicking is NOT
  in the book; in the book the phrase appears once, mid-story, as ordinary
  conversation about Kansas, and the actual command spoken is "Take me home
  to Aunt Em!"
- **The humbug's true gifts:** placebo props, but each companion had already
  demonstrated the quality all along (the Scarecrow made every plan, the
  Woodman wept for a beetle, the Lion leapt the gorge scared) — the book's
  central joke and its heart.
- **Endgame:** Glinda trades the shoe secret for the Cap, ferries the
  friends to their three kingdoms (Scarecrow rules the Emerald City, Woodman
  the Winkies, Lion his forest), and Dorothy needs nothing but what she wore
  the whole time.

## 7. Tone notes

Baum's register is matter-of-fact wonder: miracles narrated like farm
chores, and violence that is sudden, bloodless-ish, and weirdly cozy (forty
beheaded wolves are a "good fight, friend"; the Kalidahs are "dashed to
pieces" and never mentioned again; the Woodman then weeps over one beetle —
THE joke of the book, fully usable for comedy). Danger is real but recovery
is generous: everyone broken gets mended (tinsmiths, restuffing, glue at the
mender's shop). Kindness is systematically repaid (mice, Stork, Winkies) —
the book runs on debt-and-gratitude economy, which maps perfectly onto
adventure-game favors. Keep sentences plain, warm, and declarative; the
humor comes from characters being exactly what they are.

## 8. FILM TRAPS (things the design must NOT contain)

- Ruby slippers → the shoes are **SILVER**.
- "There's no place like home" as a magic formula / heel-click chant → the
  command is **"Take me home to Aunt Em!"** after knocking heels three times.
- Green-skinned Witch, broomstick, crystal ball, hourglass, "Surrender
  Dorothy" skywriting, flying on a bicycle, Miss Gulch, the farmhands, the
  traveling Professor → none exist. Book Witch: one telescopic eye, an
  umbrella, a silver whistle, fear of dark and water.
- "It was all a dream" ending → in the book Oz is real; Dorothy is simply
  home, in her stocking-feet, shoes lost in the desert.
- Munchkinland musical staging, the Lollipop Guild, Emerald City horse-of-a-
  different-color → none. Book-only selling points instead: the Deadly Poppy
  Field, the Kalidahs, the Stork, the field-mouse debt, the china country,
  the Hammer-Heads, the locked-on green spectacles, and the Golden Cap
  three-wish economy.
