# STUDY: Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (Lewis Carroll, 1865)

Source: Project Gutenberg ebook #11 (`book.txt`, Millennium Fulcrum Edition 3.0,
~27k words, 12 chapters). Bonus source: Through the Looking-Glass, ebook #12
(`looking-glass.txt`) — raided for Jabberwocky, Tweedledum/Tweedledee, and
Humpty Dumpty material. Both public domain; quote freely.

Every location, character, object, size change, and poem below was harvested
directly from the text, with line references into book.txt.

## 1. Location inventory (in book order)

### 1.1 The riverbank (ch. I opening; ch. XII close)
Alice sits "by her sister on the bank ... the hot day made her feel very
sleepy and stupid," debating "whether the pleasure of making a daisy-chain
would be worth the trouble of getting up and picking the daisies." The book
ends here too: "she found herself lying on the bank, with her head in the lap
of her sister, who was gently brushing away some dead leaves that had
fluttered down from the trees upon her face."
- Key quote: "what is the use of a book without pictures or conversations?"
- The White Rabbit runs past here: "Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be late!" and
  "actually _took a watch out of its waistcoat-pocket_."
- It pops "down a large rabbit-hole under the hedge."

### 1.2 The rabbit hole / the fall (ch. I)
"The rabbit-hole went straight on like a tunnel for some way, and then dipped
suddenly down." The well walls are "filled with cupboards and book-shelves;
here and there she saw maps and pictures hung upon pegs." She takes down a jar
"labelled 'ORANGE MARMALADE', but to her great disappointment it was empty,"
and puts it back in a cupboard as she falls. Monologue material during the
fall: "I wonder how many miles I've fallen by this time? ... somewhere near
the centre of the earth"; "Do cats eat bats? Do cats eat bats? ... Do bats eat
cats?"; the Antipathies; curtseying while falling. Ends: "thump! thump! down
she came upon a heap of sticks and dry leaves, and the fall was over." Not a
bit hurt.

### 1.3 Long passage and the hall of doors (ch. I-II)
After the heap of leaves: "another long passage, and the White Rabbit was
still in sight, hurrying down it" — he says "Oh my ears and whiskers, how late
it's getting!" and vanishes round a corner. Then: "a long, low hall, which was
lit up by a row of lamps hanging from the roof. There were doors all round the
hall, but they were all locked."
- The glass table: "a little three-legged table, all made of solid glass;
  there was nothing on it except a tiny golden key."
- "either the locks were too large, or the key was too small" — the key opens
  none of the big doors.
- "a low curtain she had not noticed before, and behind it was a little door
  about fifteen inches high" — the golden key fits it.
- Through it: "a small passage, not much larger than a rat-hole ... into the
  loveliest garden you ever saw ... beds of bright flowers and those cool
  fountains." She can't even get her head through.
- "Oh, how I wish I could shut up like a telescope!"
- DRINK ME bottle appears on the table on her second visit ("which certainly
  was not here before") — label "beautifully printed ... in large letters."
  Taste: "a sort of mixed flavour of cherry-tart, custard, pine-apple, roast
  turkey, toffee, and hot buttered toast."
- The glass box under the table holds the EAT ME cake, "the words 'EAT ME'
  ... beautifully marked in currants."
- The safety lecture: she checks the bottle isn't "marked 'poison'," because
  "if you drink much from a bottle marked 'poison,' it is almost certain to
  disagree with you, sooner or later."
- Critical sequencing tragedy (the game's tutorial): she shrinks FIRST, and
  "when she went back to the table for it, she found she could not possibly
  reach it: she could see it quite plainly through the glass ... the poor
  little thing sat down and cried."
- Later, after the fan-shrink: "the little door was shut again, and the little
  golden key was lying on the glass table as before" — Carroll himself
  re-locks the door and resets the puzzle.

### 1.4 The pool of tears (ch. II)
Made while she is nine feet tall: "shedding gallons of tears, until there was
a large pool all round her, about four inches deep and reaching half down the
hall." When small again her foot slips: "splash! she was up to her chin in
salt water ... she soon made out that she was in the pool of tears which she
had wept when she was nine feet high."
- "I shall be punished for it now, I suppose, by being drowned in my own
  tears! That _will_ be a queer thing, to be sure!"
- The Mouse swims by ("a walrus or hippopotamus" at first sight, given her
  size). She addresses it "O Mouse, do you know the way out of this pool?"
  (from her brother's Latin Grammar: "A mouse—of a mouse—to a mouse—a
  mouse—O mouse!") and, disastrously, in French: "Où est ma chatte?"
- The pool fills with creatures: "a Duck and a Dodo, a Lory and an Eaglet,
  and several other curious creatures. Alice led the way, and the whole party
  swam to the shore."

### 1.5 The Caucus-race shore (ch. III)
"They were indeed a queer-looking party that assembled on the bank—the birds
with draggled feathers, the animals with their fur clinging close to them, and
all dripping wet, cross, and uncomfortable."
- The Mouse's "dry" lecture: "'William the Conqueror, whose cause was favoured
  by the pope...' This is the driest thing I know."
- The Dodo proposes the Caucus-race: "the best way to explain it is to do it"
  — a circle course, "they began running when they liked, and left off when
  they liked," and after half an hour "The race is over!" — "_Everybody_ has
  won, and all must have prizes."
- Alice hands round comfits from her pocket, "exactly one a-piece"; her own
  prize is her own thimble, presented back by the Dodo: "We beg your
  acceptance of this elegant thimble."
- The Mouse's tale/tail — see Poems. Alice's mistake: "you had got to the
  fifth bend, I think?" / "I had _not_!" / "A knot! ... Oh, do let me help to
  undo it!" — the Mouse stalks off.
- Mentioning Dinah ("she'll eat a little bird as soon as look at it!") clears
  the shore instantly — Magpie: "the night-air doesn't suit my throat!"

### 1.6 The White Rabbit's house (ch. IV)
The Rabbit returns hunting his things: "The Duchess! The Duchess! Oh my dear
paws! Oh my fur and whiskers! She'll get me executed, as sure as ferrets are
ferrets! Where _can_ I have dropped them, I wonder?" He mistakes Alice for his
housemaid: "Why, Mary Ann, what _are_ you doing out here? Run home this
moment, and fetch me a pair of gloves and a fan! Quick, now!"
- The house: "a neat little house, on the door of which was a bright brass
  plate with the name 'W. RABBIT,' engraved upon it."
- Upstairs, "a tidy little room with a table in the window, and on it (as she
  had hoped) a fan and two or three pairs of tiny white kid gloves," plus an
  unlabeled little bottle by the looking-glass: "I know _something_
  interesting is sure to happen ... whenever I eat or drink anything."
- She grows to fill the room: "one arm out of the window, and one foot up the
  chimney" — trapped.
- Pat digging for apples ("Sure, it's an arm, yer honour!" — "arrum"); Bill
  the Lizard sent down the chimney; Alice's "one sharp kick" — "There goes
  Bill!" ... "something comes at me like a Jack-in-the-box, and up I goes like
  a sky-rocket!"
- "We must burn the house down!" / "If you do, I'll set Dinah at you!"
- The barrowful of pebbles through the window: "the pebbles were all turning
  into little cakes as they lay on the floor" — eating one shrinks her; she
  escapes past "quite a crowd of little animals" (Bill held up by two
  guinea-pigs, "giving it something out of a bottle").

### 1.7 The wood and the enormous puppy (ch. IV)
Tiny Alice in a thick wood. "An enormous puppy was looking down at her with
large round eyes, and feebly stretching out one paw, trying to touch her."
She holds out a stick; the puppy charges it repeatedly ("a series of short
charges at the stick"); she hides behind "a great thistle" and escapes when it
tires. "And yet what a dear little puppy it was! ... I should have liked
teaching it tricks very much, if—if I'd only been the right size to do it!"

### 1.8 The Caterpillar's mushroom (ch. IV-V)
"There was a large mushroom growing near her, about the same height as
herself ... her eyes immediately met those of a large blue caterpillar, that
was sitting on the top with its arms folded, quietly smoking a long hookah,
and taking not the smallest notice of her or of anything else." (She is three
inches tall here; so is he — exactly.)

### 1.9 The treetops / the Pigeon (ch. V)
After the left-hand bite, her neck telescopes: "an immense length of neck,
which seemed to rise like a stalk out of a sea of green leaves." The Pigeon
attacks: "Serpent!" — "I'm _not_ a serpent!" — "You're a serpent; and there's
no use denying it. I suppose you'll be telling me next that you never tasted
an egg!" — "I _have_ tasted eggs, certainly ... but little girls eat eggs
quite as much as serpents do, you know." — "if they do, why then they're a
kind of serpent, that's all I can say."

### 1.10 The Duchess's house and kitchen (ch. VI)
"a little house in it about four feet high" — she nibbles down to nine inches
before approaching. The Fish-Footman delivers to the Frog-Footman: "For the
Duchess. An invitation from the Queen to play croquet." (Repeated back with
the words reshuffled.) "they both bowed low, and their curls got entangled
together."
- Frog-Footman on knocking: "There's no sort of use in knocking ... First,
  because I'm on the same side of the door as you are; secondly, because
  they're making such a noise inside, no one could possibly hear you." And:
  "I shall sit here, on and off, for days and days."
- "_Are_ you to get in at all? That's the first question, you know."
- Alice's move: "And she opened the door and went in."
- Kitchen: "full of smoke from one end to the other: the Duchess was sitting
  on a three-legged stool in the middle, nursing a baby; the cook was leaning
  over the fire, stirring a large cauldron which seemed to be full of soup.
  'There's certainly too much pepper in that soup!'" Everyone sneezes except
  the cook and the grinning cat.
- Cook throws "the fire-irons ... then followed a shower of saucepans,
  plates, and dishes" at the Duchess and baby.
- "Talking of axes," said the Duchess, "chop off her head!"
- The lullaby: "Speak roughly to your little boy..." (see Poems).
- "Here! you may nurse it a bit, if you like!" — she flings the baby at Alice
  and leaves "to get ready to play croquet with the Queen."
- The baby ("a queer-shaped little creature ... just like a star-fish";
  nursing method: "twist it up into a sort of knot, and then keep tight hold
  of its right ear and left foot") grunts, develops "a _very_ turn-up nose,
  much more like a snout," and becomes a pig: "it would have made a
  dreadfully ugly child: but it makes rather a handsome pig, I think."

### 1.11 The Cheshire Cat's tree (ch. VI)
The Cat on a bough — full hint-giver dialogue in the roster below. It points
the ways: "In _that_ direction lives a Hatter: and in _that_ direction lives
a March Hare. Visit either you like: they're both mad." Vanishing: "this time
it vanished quite slowly, beginning with the end of the tail, and ending with
the grin, which remained some time after the rest of it had gone." — "a grin
without a cat! It's the most curious thing I ever saw in my life!"

### 1.12 The March Hare's tea table (ch. VII)
The house: "the chimneys were shaped like ears and the roof was thatched with
fur" — so large she nibbles up to two feet first. "There was a table set out
under a tree in front of the house, and the March Hare and the Hatter were
having tea at it: a Dormouse was sitting between them, fast asleep, and the
other two were using it as a cushion."
- "No room! No room!" / "There's _plenty_ of room!"
- Full tea-party dialogue in roster. It is "always six o'clock" — "it's
  always tea-time, and we've no time to wash the things between whiles" —
  they perpetually move round the table.
- The treacle-well story: Elsie, Lacie, and Tillie "lived at the bottom of a
  well ... It was a treacle-well ... they were learning to draw ... they drew
  all manner of things—everything that begins with an M ... mouse-traps, and
  the moon, and memory, and muchness."
- Exit: "the last time she saw them, they were trying to put the Dormouse
  into the teapot." — "It's the stupidest tea-party I ever was at in all my
  life!"

### 1.13 The tree with a door (ch. VII)
"she noticed that one of the trees had a door leading right into it. 'That's
very curious!' ... And in she went. Once more she found herself in the long
hall, and close to the little glass table." This time she does it RIGHT:
"began by taking the little golden key, and unlocking the door that led into
the garden. Then she went to work nibbling at the mushroom (she had kept a
piece of it in her pocket) till she was about a foot high: then she walked
down the little passage: and _then_—she found herself at last in the
beautiful garden, among the bright flower-beds and the cool fountains."
UNLOCK FIRST, THEN SHRINK — the book's own stated solution.

### 1.14 The Queen's garden and croquet-ground (ch. VIII)
"A large rose-tree stood near the entrance of the garden: the roses growing
on it were white, but there were three gardeners at it, busily painting them
red." Gardeners Two, Five, and Seven (playing cards, "oblong and flat, with
their hands and feet at the corners"): "this here ought to have been a _red_
rose-tree, and we put a white one in by mistake; and if the Queen was to find
it out, we should all have our heads cut off."
- The procession: ten soldiers with clubs, ten courtiers in diamonds, ten
  royal children in hearts, guests, the White Rabbit, the Knave of Hearts
  carrying the crown on a crimson cushion, then THE KING AND QUEEN OF HEARTS.
- "Why, they're only a pack of cards, after all. I needn't be afraid of them!"
- Queen: "Off with her head! Off—" / Alice: "Nonsense!" — "and the Queen was
  silent." King: "Consider, my dear: she is only a child!"
- Alice hides the condemned gardeners "into a large flower-pot"; the soldiers
  report "Their heads are gone, if it please your Majesty!" — "That's right!"
- The croquet ground: "all ridges and furrows; the balls were live hedgehogs,
  the mallets live flamingoes, and the soldiers had to double themselves up
  ... to make the arches." The flamingo "_would_ twist itself round and look
  up in her face"; the hedgehog unrolls and crawls away; the arches walk off.
  "The players all played at once without waiting for turns."
- Queen executes everyone verbally ("about once in a minute"); the Gryphon's
  secret: "It's all her fancy, that: they never executes nobody, you know."
  The King, sotto voce: "You are all pardoned."
- The Cheshire Cat's head appears; King: "it may kiss my hand if it likes." /
  Cat: "I'd rather not." / Alice: "A cat may look at a king." The beheading
  dispute: executioner — "you couldn't cut off a head unless there was a body
  to cut it off from"; King — "anything that had a head could be beheaded";
  Queen — "if something wasn't done about it in less than no time she'd have
  everybody executed, all round." Alice: "It belongs to the Duchess: you'd
  better ask _her_ about it."

### 1.15 The Duchess's promenade (ch. IX)
The freed Duchess, arm-in-arm, chin digging into Alice's shoulder, finds a
moral in everything: "Everything's got a moral, if only you can find it";
"'Oh, 'tis love, 'tis love, that makes the world go round!'"; "Take care of
the sense, and the sounds will take care of themselves"; "flamingoes and
mustard both bite ... Birds of a feather flock together"; "there's a large
mustard-mine near here. And the moral of that is—'The more there is of mine,
the less there is of yours'"; and the monstrous "Be what you would seem to
be" sentence. The Queen appears: "either you or your head must be off, and
that in about half no time! Take your choice!" The Duchess takes her choice.

### 1.16 The seaside: Gryphon and Mock Turtle (ch. IX-X)
"a Gryphon, lying fast asleep in the sun." Queen: "Up, lazy thing! ... take
this young lady to see the Mock Turtle, and to hear his history." The Mock
Turtle sits "sad and lonely on a little ledge of rock ... sighing as if his
heart would break." ("It's the thing Mock Turtle Soup is made from," said the
Queen.)
- "Once, I was a real Turtle." School in the sea: master "an old Turtle—we
  used to call him Tortoise" — "because he taught us"; subjects: "Reeling and
  Writhing ... Ambition, Distraction, Uglification, and Derision"; "Mystery,
  ancient and modern, with Seaography: then Drawling ... Drawling,
  Stretching, and Fainting in Coils"; the Classics master "taught Laughing
  and Grief." Lessons "lessen from day to day."
- The Lobster Quadrille (dance + song — see Poems). "Change lobsters, and
  retire in same order ... you throw the lobsters as far out to sea as you
  can—" "Swim after them!" "Turn a somersault in the sea!"
- The whiting: "It does the boots and shoes ... Boots and shoes under the
  sea are done with a whiting"; made of "soles and eels"; "no wise fish would
  go anywhere without a porpoise ... 'With what porpoise?'"
- "Turtle Soup" song; interrupted by "The trial's beginning!" — the Gryphon
  grabs her hand and runs.

### 1.17 The courtroom (ch. XI-XII)
"The King and Queen of Hearts were seated on their throne ... the Knave was
standing before them, in chains, with a soldier on each side to guard him;
and near the King was the White Rabbit, with a trumpet in one hand, and a
scroll of parchment in the other. In the very middle of the court was a
table, with a large dish of tarts upon it."
- Twelve jurors on slates "putting down their names, for fear they should
  forget them before the end of the trial." Alice confiscates Bill the
  Lizard's squeaky pencil; he writes with one finger thereafter.
- The accusation: "The Queen of Hearts, she made some tarts..." (see Poems).
- Witness 1, the Hatter (teacup in hand, bites the cup instead of the
  bread-and-butter): "I'm a poor man, your Majesty ... and the twinkling of
  the tea—" King: "Of course twinkling begins with a T! Do you take me for a
  dunce?" — "If that's all you know about it, you may stand down." "I can't
  go no lower, I'm on the floor, as it is." "Then you may _sit_ down." He
  flees without his shoes; the Queen adds "just take his head off outside."
- Guinea-pigs cheer and are "suppressed" (canvas bag, sat upon).
- Witness 2, the cook (pepper-box; everyone near the door sneezes): "What
  are tarts made of?" / "Pepper, mostly." / A sleepy voice: "Treacle." — the
  Queen: "Collar that Dormouse! Behead that Dormouse! ... Off with his
  whiskers!" The cook disappears in the confusion.
- Witness 3: "Alice!" She has been growing throughout ("You've no right to
  grow _here_," says the Dormouse; "Don't talk nonsense; you know you're
  growing too." "Yes, but _I_ grow at a reasonable pace"). She tips over the
  jury-box (goldfish-globe memory; puts the Lizard back head-upwards).
- "Rule Forty-two. _All persons more than a mile high to leave the court_."
  "_I'm_ not a mile high." / "You are." / "Nearly two miles high." / "that's
  not a regular rule: you invented it just now." / "It's the oldest rule in
  the book." / "Then it ought to be Number One."
- The unsigned verses ("They told me you had been to her..."): "If you
  didn't sign it, that only makes the matter worse. You _must_ have meant
  some mischief, or else you'd have signed your name like an honest man."
  Alice: "I'll give him sixpence. _I_ don't believe there's an atom of
  meaning in it." King finds meaning anyway: "'—_said I could not swim_—'
  you can't swim, can you?" — the Knave, "made entirely of cardboard," shakes
  his head sadly: "Do I look like it?"
- The King's pun: "Then the words don't _fit_ you" — dead silence — "It's a
  pun!" — everybody laughs.
- Finale: "Let the jury consider their verdict." / Queen: "No, no! Sentence
  first—verdict afterwards." / Alice: "Stuff and nonsense! The idea of having
  the sentence first!" / "Off with her head!" Nobody moved. / "Who cares for
  you? You're nothing but a pack of cards!" — "the whole pack rose up into
  the air, and came flying down upon her" — and she wakes on the bank.
- Coda: "Wake up, Alice dear! Why, what a long sleep you've had!" ... "run
  in to your tea; it's getting late." Then the sister's reverie mapping every
  Wonderland sound to a real one (teacups → sheep-bells, Queen's cries → the
  shepherd boy, Mock Turtle's sobs → lowing cattle).

## 2. Character roster: voices and reusable dialogue

### White Rabbit (the through-line)
Fussy, terrified of the Duchess and the Queen, self-important with inferiors.
- "Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be late!"
- "Oh my ears and whiskers, how late it's getting!"
- "Oh! the Duchess, the Duchess! Oh! won't she be savage if I've kept her
  waiting!"
- "She'll get me executed, as sure as ferrets are ferrets!"
- "Why, Mary Ann, what _are_ you doing out here? Run home this moment, and
  fetch me a pair of gloves and a fan! Quick, now!"
- Court herald: three trumpet blasts; "Silence in the court!"; corrects the
  King in terror: "_Un_important, your Majesty means, of course."
- Small talk at croquet: "It's—it's a very fine day!" and the whispered
  gossip: "She's under sentence of execution." ("She boxed the Queen's
  ears—")

### The Mouse
Dignified, touchy, French-adjacent, cat-traumatized.
- "Not like cats! Would _you_ like cats if you were me?"
- "Our family always _hated_ cats: nasty, low, vulgar things! Don't let me
  hear the name again!"
- The dry lecture: "This is the driest thing I know. Silence all round, if
  you please! 'William the Conqueror, whose cause was favoured by the
  pope...'"
- "Mine is a long and a sad tale!" — "It _is_ a long tail, certainly ... but
  why do you call it sad?"
- "I had _not_!" / "A knot! Oh, do let me help to undo it!" / "You insult me
  by talking such nonsense!"

### The Dodo
Pompous parliamentary diction, secretly kind.
- "I move that the meeting adjourn, for the immediate adoption of more
  energetic remedies—" (Eaglet: "Speak English! I don't know the meaning of
  half those long words, and, what's more, I don't believe you do either!")
- "the best way to explain it is to do it."
- "_Everybody_ has won, and all must have prizes."
- "We beg your acceptance of this elegant thimble."

### The Caterpillar
Languid, contradictory, exactly three inches tall and proud of it.
- "Who are _you?_" (opening and closing move of every exchange)
- "What do you mean by that? Explain yourself!" / "I don't see." / "It
  isn't." / "Not a bit." / "Why?"
- "Come back! I've something important to say! ... Keep your temper."
- "Can't remember _what_ things?" / "Repeat, '_You are old, Father
  William_.'" / verdict: "That is not said right ... It is wrong from
  beginning to end."
- "What size do you want to be?" ... "It is a very good height indeed!"
  (angrily, rearing to its full three inches)
- Exit line, the item drop: "One side will make you grow taller, and the
  other side will make you grow shorter." — "One side of _what?_" — "Of the
  mushroom," said the Caterpillar, just as if she had asked it aloud.

### The Pigeon
Hysterical maternal logic. "Serpent!" / "I've tried the roots of trees, and
I've tried banks, and I've tried hedges ... but those serpents! There's no
pleasing them!" / "You're looking for eggs, I know _that_ well enough."

### The Frog-Footman
Immovable philosophical obstruction.
- "There's no sort of use in knocking, and that for two reasons..."
- "_Are_ you to get in at all? That's the first question, you know."
- "I shall sit here, on and off, for days and days."
- "Anything you like," said the Footman, and began whistling.
(A plate skims out of the door and grazes his nose; "—or next day, maybe,"
he continues, exactly as if nothing had happened.)

### The Duchess
Two moods: pepper (savage) and no-pepper (cloyingly moralizing).
- Kitchen: "It's a Cheshire cat, and that's why. Pig!"; "You don't know
  much, and that's a fact."; "If everybody minded their own business, the
  world would go round a deal faster than it does."; "Talking of axes, chop
  off her head!"; "Here! you may nurse it a bit, if you like!"
- Promenade: "Everything's got a moral, if only you can find it."; "'Tis
  love, 'tis love, that makes the world go round!"; "Take care of the sense,
  and the sounds will take care of themselves."; "flamingoes and mustard
  both bite"; "The more there is of mine, the less there is of yours."; the
  "Be what you would seem to be" run-on; "I make you a present of everything
  I've said as yet." ("A cheap sort of present!" thought Alice.)

### The Cook
Monosyllabic violence. Throws fire-irons, saucepans, plates, dishes, a
frying-pan. In court: "Give your evidence." / "Shan't." / "What are tarts
made of?" / "Pepper, mostly."

### The Cheshire Cat
The game's guide-in-fiction: calm, logical, appears/vanishes at will, grin
outlasts body.
- "We're all mad here. I'm mad. You're mad." / "You must be, or you wouldn't
  have come here."
- "That depends a good deal on where you want to get to." / "Then it doesn't
  matter which way you go." / "Oh, you're sure to do that, if you only walk
  long enough."
- "In _that_ direction lives a Hatter: and in _that_ direction lives a March
  Hare. Visit either you like: they're both mad."
- The madness proof: "a dog growls when it's angry, and wags its tail when
  it's pleased. Now _I_ growl when I'm pleased, and wag my tail when I'm
  angry. Therefore I'm mad."
- "Did you say pig, or fig?"
- "You'll see me there," said the Cat, and vanished.
- "How are you getting on?" (head-only, at croquet); to the King: "I'd
  rather not." (re: kissing his hand)

### The Hatter
Abrupt, personal, time-haunted.
- "Your hair wants cutting."
- "Why is a raven like a writing-desk?" ... "I haven't the slightest idea."
- "It was the _best_ butter, you know." (March Hare's line, but the watch is
  the Hatter's: "Two days wrong! I told you butter wouldn't suit the works!"
  "Yes, but some crumbs must have got in as well: you shouldn't have put it
  in with the bread-knife.")
- "If you knew Time as well as I do, you wouldn't talk about wasting _it_.
  It's _him_." / "He won't stand beating."
- The quarrel: sang "Twinkle, twinkle, little bat" at the Queen's concert;
  "He's murdering the time! Off with his head!" — "And ever since that, he
  won't do a thing I ask! It's always six o'clock now."
- "Take some more tea." / "I've had nothing yet, so I can't take more." /
  "You mean you can't take _less_: it's very easy to take _more_ than
  nothing."
- "I want a clean cup: let's all move one place on."
- "Then you shouldn't talk."
- In court: "I'm a poor man, your Majesty"; "I can't go no lower, I'm on the
  floor, as it is"; bites teacup; flees shoeless.

### The March Hare
- "Have some wine." (there isn't any) / "It wasn't very civil of you to sit
  down without being invited."
- "Then you should say what you mean." / "You might just as well say that
  'I like what I get' is the same thing as 'I get what I like'!"
- "It was the _best_ butter." / "Suppose we change the subject."
- In court: "I deny it!"

### The Dormouse
Sleep-talking narrative engine.
- "You might just as well say that 'I breathe when I sleep' is the same
  thing as 'I sleep when I breathe'!"
- "I wasn't asleep: I heard every word you fellows were saying."
- The treacle-well story: Elsie, Lacie, Tillie; "They lived on treacle";
  "So they were, _very_ ill"; "It was a treacle-well"; "—well in"; drawing
  "everything that begins with an M ... did you ever see such a thing as a
  drawing of a muchness?"
- In court: "Treacle," (a sleepy voice); and to growing Alice: "I wish you
  wouldn't squeeze so. I can hardly breathe." / "You've no right to grow
  _here_." / "_I_ grow at a reasonable pace, not in that ridiculous fashion."

### The Queen of Hearts
One idea, at volume. Never actually gets anyone executed (Gryphon: "they
never executes nobody, you know"; King pardons all).
- "Off with her head!" / "Off with his head!" (about once a minute)
- "Who is this? ... What's your name, child?" / "And who are _these_?"
- "Get to your places!" (voice of thunder) / "Can you play croquet?"
- "Are their heads off?"
- "either you or your head must be off, and that in about half no time!
  Take your choice!"
- "Have you seen the Mock Turtle yet? ... It's the thing Mock Turtle Soup is
  made from."
- "Sentence first—verdict afterwards." / "Collar that Dormouse! ... Off with
  his whiskers!"

### The King of Hearts
Mild, henpecked, judicial nonsense.
- "Consider, my dear: she is only a child!"
- "it may kiss my hand if it likes." / "Don't be impertinent ... and don't
  look at me like that!"
- "anything that had a head could be beheaded."
- Judge: wig with crown over it. "Consider your verdict" (about the
  twentieth time that day); "Give your evidence, and don't be nervous, or
  I'll have you executed on the spot."; "Of course twinkling begins with a
  T! Do you take me for a dunce?"; "He denies it: leave out that part.";
  "You're a _very_ poor _speaker_."; "Rule Forty-two"; "If you didn't sign
  it, that only makes the matter worse"; "Begin at the beginning, and go on
  till you come to the end: then stop."; "Then the words don't _fit_ you ...
  It's a pun!"; and, quietly, "You are all pardoned."

### The Gryphon
Slangy, cheerful, drags Alice everywhere.
- "What fun! ... It's all her fancy, that: they never executes nobody, you
  know. Come on!"
- "This here young lady, she wants for to know your history, she do."
- "Hjckrrh!" / "Hold your tongue!" / "Drive on, old fellow! Don't be all day
  about it!"
- "That's the reason they're called lessons, because they lessen from day to
  day."
- "_It does the boots and shoes_." / "Soles and eels, of course: any shrimp
  could have told you that."
- "The trial's beginning! Come on!"

### The Mock Turtle
Lachrymose ex-turtle, sighs and sobs between clauses.
- "Once, I was a real Turtle."
- "We called him Tortoise because he taught us: really you are very dull!"
- "Reeling and Writhing ... Ambition, Distraction, Uglification, and
  Derision."
- "no wise fish would go anywhere without a porpoise ... 'With what
  porpoise?'" / "I mean what I say."
- Sings the Lobster Quadrille and "Beautiful Soup."

### The gardeners: Two, Five, Seven
Bickering playing-cards. "Look out now, Five! Don't go splashing paint over
me like that!" / "Seven jogged my elbow." / "That's right, Five! Always lay
the blame on others!" / "this here ought to have been a _red_ rose-tree, and
we put a white one in by mistake; and if the Queen was to find it out, we
should all have our heads cut off." / (flat on faces) "The Queen! The Queen!"

### The Knave of Hearts
In chains; cardboard. "Please your Majesty, I didn't write it, and they
can't prove I did: there's no name signed at the end." / "Do I look like
it?" (re: swimming)

### Bill the Lizard
Put-upon everylizard. Sent down the chimney, kicked sky-high ("up I goes
like a sky-rocket!"), juror with squeaky pencil (confiscated, writes with a
finger), put back in the jury-box head downward, inkstand thrown at him.

### Minor: Pat ("Sure, it's an arm, yer honour!" — "arrum"; "Digging for

## 4. THE SIZE MECHANIC — every change, cause, effect

This is the engine of the whole book. Complete table, in order:

| # | Cause | From → To | Enabled | Blocked / cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DRINK ME bottle (hall) | normal → 10 inches | right size for the little door | key now unreachable on glass table (she drank before unlocking — sequencing failure #1); bottle is finished |
| 2 | EAT ME cake (hall) | 10 in → 9+ feet (head hits roof) | can take the key easily | far too big for the door (sequencing failure #2); cries the 4-inch-deep pool of tears |
| 3 | The Rabbit's fan (held/fanning) | 9 ft → ~2 ft and shrinking fast | escape from giant-hood | nearly shrinks "out altogether, like a candle" — drops fan just in time; door shut AGAIN, key back on table; slips into her own pool |
| 4 | Unlabeled bottle (Rabbit's house) | small → fills the room | (nothing—pure trap) | trapped: arm out window, foot up chimney; can't leave |
| 5 | Pebble-cake (Rabbit's house) | room-size → small enough for door | escape the house | now tiny in a world of enormous puppies |
| 6 | Mushroom, right-hand bite | small → chin-strikes-foot tiny | (overshoot demo) | almost can't open mouth for the counter-bite |
| 7 | Mushroom, left-hand bite | tiny → neck above the treetops | (overshoot demo) | mistaken for a serpent by the Pigeon; hands lost below |
| 8 | Alternating nibbles | → usual height | calibration mastered; "half my plan done" | — |
| 9 | Right-hand nibble (before Duchess's) | normal → 9 inches | can approach/enter the 4-ft house | — |
| 10 | Left-hand nibble (before March Hare's) | 9 in → 2 feet | dares approach the large house | — |
| 11 | Mushroom nibbles (hall, second visit) | → about 1 foot | THE SOLUTION: unlock first at reachable-key size, then shrink, walk into the garden | — |
| 12 | Spontaneous growth (courtroom) | small → full size+ ("nearly two miles high" per the Queen) | courage: tips jury-box, defies Rule 42, "Stuff and nonsense!", "You're nothing but a pack of cards!" | squeezes the Dormouse; Rule 42 invoked |

Design extraction:
- Size is a GLOBAL STATE the whole world reads: exits (little door, 4-ft
  house), reach (key on table), threat scale (puppy), social standing
  (Pigeon's taxonomy, Dormouse's complaint), and even self (serpent neck).
- Consumables are ONE-SHOT and appear in the wrong order — that IS the
  tutorial. The mushroom converts the mechanic from scripted tragedy into a
  reusable player tool. Carroll even states the solved order aloud (unlock,
  THEN shrink).
- The fan is a "shrink while held" hazard-item with a near-death comedy beat.
- Growth inside a container (Rabbit's house) is a trap state with its own
  escape item delivered BY the besiegers (pebble-cakes).

## 7. Notes for adaptation

- Carroll's plot is a picaresque chained by ONE goal Alice states twice:
  "The first thing I've got to do is to grow to my right size again; and the
  second thing is to find my way into that lovely garden." That is the game
  spine, verbatim.
- The Rabbit appears at every act break (riverbank, hall, house errand,
  procession, herald) — a natural trail-of-breadcrumbs NPC.
- The book is stuffed with command-shaped sentences ("Keep your temper,"
  "Take some more tea," "Off with her head!", "Consider your verdict") that
  make perfect parser responses.
- Deaths never happen: every execution is fancy, every drowning a swim,
  every fall a soft landing. A forgiving game is CANON.
- The tail-poem's visual joke must become an ear joke (the "fifth bend"/"a
  knot!" exchange already IS the ear version — use that).
- Repeated Carroll engine: politeness works, logic backfires, temper loses.
  Good axis for NPC interaction rules.
