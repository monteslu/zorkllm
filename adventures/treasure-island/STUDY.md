# TREASURE ISLAND — study notes

Source: Project Gutenberg ebook #120 (`book.txt`, ~71,700 words, 34 chapters
in six parts). All quotes below are verbatim from that text and are public
domain. Line numbers refer to book.txt.

---

## 1. Complete location inventory (in order of appearance)

### Act-of-the-book I — the West Country coast

**The Admiral Benbow inn, Black Hill Cove.** Jim's home; his father keeps it.
Rooms mentioned: the parlour ("a corner of the parlour next the fire"), the
bar (with the till and the rum tap), the kitchen (grate of coals), the
captain's room upstairs ("the little room where he had slept so long and
where his box had stood since the day of his arrival"), the door with its
bolt, and the big signboard outside — Black Dog's escape is saved by "our big
signboard of Admiral Benbow. You may see the notch on the lower side of the
frame to this day." Atmosphere: "On stormy nights, when the wind shook the
four corners of the house and the surf roared along the cove and up the
cliffs..." The inn is chosen by Billy Bones because it is "a handy cove...
and a pleasant sittyated grog-shop. Much company, mate?" — i.e. lonely.

**The cove and cliffs.** Billy "all day... hung round the cove or upon the
cliffs with a brass telescope," watching for seafaring men. A "big rock" on
the beach he strides past.

**The road** past the inn, along which Pew comes "tap-tapping... upon the
frozen road," and the **little bridge** near the inn: "We were just at the
little bridge, by good fortune... I managed to drag her down the bank and a
little way under the arch. Farther I could not move her, for the bridge was
too low to let me do more than crawl below it." Jim watches Pew's end from
"behind a bush of broom."

**The hamlet** — "not many hundred yards away, though out of view, on the
other side of the next cove." The cowardly neighbours: "not one would help
us to defend the inn." They lend Jim a loaded pistol.

**Kitt's Hole** — the smugglers' cove where the pirates' lugger waits.

**Dr. Livesey's house** and **the squire's hall**: "a great library, all
lined with bookcases and busts upon the top of them, where the squire and
Dr. Livesey sat, pipe in hand, on either side of a bright fire."

### Act-of-the-book II — Bristol

**Bristol docks / the quays.** "our way... lay along the quays and beside
the great multitude of ships of all sizes and rigs and nations... The smell
of tar and salt was something new. I saw the most wonderful figureheads."
The squire lodges at an inn "far down the docks" (the *Old Anchor* in his
letter).

**The Spy-glass tavern** — Silver's public house, "a little tavern with a
large brass telescope for sign... a bright enough little place of
entertainment. The sign was newly painted; the windows had neat red
curtains; the floor was cleanly sanded. There was a street on each side and
an open door on both... clouds of tobacco smoke." Black Dog is drinking here
and bolts when Jim walks in.

### The Hispaniola (schooner, two hundred tons)

- The **deck/waist**: "always a barrel of apples standing broached in the
  waist for anyone to help himself." Amidships a swivel gun, "a long brass
  nine" (Long Tom).
- The **galley**: Silver "kept as clean as a new pin, the dishes hanging up
  burnished and his parrot in a cage in one corner."
- The **cabin** astern: six berths made astern; powder and arms moved there
  by Smollett's order. Stern window opens onto the wake. After the mutiny it
  is a wreck: "All the lockfast places had been broken open in quest of the
  chart... Dozens of empty bottles clinked together in corners... One of the
  doctor's medical books lay open on the table, half of the leaves gutted
  out, I suppose, for pipelights."
- The **sparred gallery/companion** connecting cabin to forecastle; the
  **fore companion**; the **cellar** (spirits store); the **cross-trees** of
  the mizzen mast (Hands duel); the **bowsprit/jib-boom** (how Jim boards
  from the coracle); the **water-breaker** beside the fore companion.

### Act-of-the-book III–VI — Treasure Island

Map facts: "about nine miles long and five across, shaped, you might say,
like a fat dragon standing up, and had two fine land-locked harbours, and a
hill in the centre part marked 'The Spy-glass.'" Three red crosses — two in
the north, one southwest with "Bulk of treasure here."

- **Captain Kidd's Anchorage** (southern anchorage) behind **Skeleton
  Island**: "The place was entirely land-locked, buried in woods, the trees
  coming right down to high-water mark... A peculiar stagnant smell hung
  over the anchorage — a smell of sodden leaves and rotting tree trunks."
  Livesey: "I don't know about treasure, but I'll stake my wig there's
  fever here."
- **The marsh / swamp**: "a marshy tract full of willows, bulrushes, and
  odd, outlandish, swampy trees"; ducks fly up in clouds when men approach;
  Silver murders Tom in "a little green dell beside the marsh." Rattlesnakes:
  "one raised his head from a ledge of rock and hissed at me... Little did I
  suppose that he was a deadly enemy and that the noise was the famous
  rattle."
- **The open woods**: live oaks "grew low along the sand like brambles,"
  scattered pines fifty to seventy feet high.
- **Spy-glass Hill**: "by three or four hundred feet the tallest on the
  island... running up sheer from almost every side and then suddenly cut
  off at the top like a pedestal to put a statue on." The three hills in a
  row: "fore, main, and mizzen" masts; Foremast Hill to the north,
  Mizzenmast Hill south.
- **The two-peaked hill** (the "two-pointed hill," NE angle of the island):
  where Jim meets Ben Gunn; a spout of falling gravel announces him. Nearby
  Ben's goat "cetemery" where he prays; goats "all mastheaded on them
  mountings for the fear of Benjamin Gunn."
- **The stockade**: "a spring of clear water rose almost at the top of a
  knoll. Well, on the knoll, and enclosing the spring, they had clapped a
  stout loghouse fit to hold two score of people on a pinch and loopholed
  for musketry on either side... a paling six feet high, without door or
  opening, too strong to pull down without time and labour and too open to
  shelter the besiegers." Built years before by Flint. The **log-house**:
  unsquared pine trunks, porch, spring welling into "a great ship's kettle
  of iron, with the bottom knocked out"; sand everywhere: "sand in our eyes,
  sand in our teeth, sand in our suppers"; a square hole in the roof for a
  chimney; loopholes: two east, two west, two south at the porch, five north.
- **The sandy spit** east of the anchorage, "joined at half-water to
  Skeleton Island," and on it the **white rock**: "an isolated rock, pretty
  high, and peculiarly white in colour... Right below it there was an
  exceedingly small hollow of green turf... and in the centre of the dell,
  sure enough, a little tent of goat-skins... and there was Ben Gunn's
  boat."
- **The west coast**: **Haulbowline Head** ("bound with cliffs forty or
  fifty feet high"), sea lions ("huge slimy monsters — soft snails, as it
  were, of incredible bigness"), **Mizzenmast Hill**, **Cape of the Woods**
  "buried in tall green pines, which descended to the margin of the sea."
  A current "sets northward along the whole west coast."
- **North Inlet**: "longer and narrower and more like... the estuary of a
  river. Right before us, at the southern end, we saw the wreck of a ship in
  the last stages of dilapidation... hung about with great webs of dripping
  seaweed, and on the deck of it shore bushes had taken root and now
  flourished thick with flowers." Hands: "there's a pet bit for to beach a
  ship in. Fine flat sand, never a cat's paw, trees all around of it, and
  flowers a-blowing like a garding on that old ship."
- **The plateau** below the Spy-glass shoulder: "a heavy-scented broom and
  many flowering shrubs... Thickets of green nutmeg-trees were dotted here
  and there with the red columns and the broad shadow of the pines." Here
  lies the **skeleton** (Allardyce), and the third of three **tall trees**:
  "nearly two hundred feet into the air... a giant of a vegetable, with a
  red column as big as a cottage." Beneath it, the **cache**: "a great
  excavation, not very recent, for the sides had fallen in and grass had
  sprouted on the bottom. In this were the shaft of a pick broken in two and
  the boards of several packing-cases... branded with a hot iron, the name
  WALRUS."
- **Ben Gunn's cave** on the two-pointed hill (nearest beach = **Rum
  Cove**): "a large, airy place, with a little spring and a pool of clear
  water, overhung with ferns. The floor was sand. Before a big fire lay
  Captain Smollett; and in a far corner... great heaps of coin and
  quadrilaterals built of bars of gold."

## 2. Character roster with voice notes and reusable lines

### Jim Hawkins (the player)
First-person narrator; brave, impulsive, self-critical. His two "follies"
(going ashore, taking the coracle) both save the expedition. Defiance
speech to the pirates: "The laugh's on my side; I've had the top of this
business from the first; I no more fear you than I fear a fly."

### Billy Bones ("the captain")
"a tall, strong, heavy, nut-brown man, his tarry pigtail falling over the
shoulder of his soiled blue coat... the sabre cut across one cheek, a dirty,
livid white." Speech tics: blows through his nose "like a fog-horn."
- "This is a handy cove... and a pleasant sittyated grog-shop."
- "Rum and bacon and eggs is what I want, and that head up there for to
  watch ships off... You mought call me captain."
- Pays Jim "a silver fourpenny on the first of every month" to watch for
  "the seafaring man with one leg."
- "Doctors is all swabs... I lived on rum, I tell you. It's been meat and
  drink, and man and wife, to me."
- "I seen old Flint in the corner there, behind you; as plain as print."
- "If it comes to swinging, swing all, say I."
- On the spot: "Ten o'clock! Six hours. We'll do them yet." Then dies of
  "thundering apoplexy."
- Tattoos: "Here's luck," "A fair wind," "Billy Bones his fancy," and a
  gallows sketch. Pockets: "A few small coins, a thimble, and some thread
  and big needles, a piece of pigtail tobacco bitten away at the end, his
  gully with the crooked handle, a pocket compass, and a tinder box."
  Key "hanging to a bit of tarry string" round his neck.

### Black Dog
"a pale, tallowy creature, wanting two fingers of the left hand." Fawning
menace: "Come here, sonny... Is this here table for my mate Bill?" "This'll
be as good as drink to my mate Bill." Flees with a slashed shoulder.

### Blind Pew
"hunched, as if with age or weakness... a huge old tattered sea-cloak with a
hood that made him appear positively deformed. I never saw in my life a more
dreadful-looking figure." Sing-song beggar patter flips instantly to cruelty:
- "Will any kind friend inform a poor blind man, who has lost the precious
  sight of his eyes in the gracious defence of his native country, England
  — and God bless King George! — where or in what part of this country he
  may now be?"
- "Take me in straight or I'll break your arm."
- "If I can't see, I can hear a finger stirring."
- Directing the raid: "Down with the door!" "In, in, in!" "Scatter, lads,
  and find 'em!" "Oh, shiver my soul, if I had eyes!" "You have your hands
  on thousands, you fools, and you hang a leg!"
- Death: deserted by his crew, "he took a wrong turn... crying, 'Johnny,
  Black Dog, Dirk... you won't leave old Pew, mates — not old Pew!'" and is
  trampled by Supervisor Dance's horse.

### Dr. Livesey
Neat, cool, ironic; magistrate as well as physician. Signature oath: "I'll
stake my wig."
- To Billy: "if you keep on drinking rum, the world will soon be quit of a
  very dirty scoundrel!" and "the name of rum for you is death."
- "If there's any doubt about the matter, he is [sane]... A man who has been
  three years biting his nails on a desert island, Jim, can't expect to
  appear as sane as you or me."
- The snuff-box: "in my snuff-box I carry a piece of Parmesan cheese — a
  cheese made in Italy, very nutritious. Well, that's for Ben Gunn!"
- To Silver: "look out for squalls when you find it."
- To Jim: "As you have brewed, so shall you drink, my boy."

### Squire Trelawney
"a tall man, over six feet high... bluff, rough-and-ready face." Loud,
generous, cannot keep a secret. "Blackbeard was a child to Flint." "If we
have the clue you talk about, I fit out a ship in Bristol dock... and I'll
have that treasure if I search a year." "Sir, I could find it in my heart to
blow the ship up." To Silver at the end: "John Silver, you're a prodigious
villain and imposter — a monstrous imposter, sir... the dead men, sir, hang
about your neck like mill-stones." Best shot of the party.

### Captain Smollett
Dry, sharp, honest. "I don't like this cruise; I don't like the men; and I
don't like my officer. That's short and sweet." "Treasure is ticklish work."
To the mutineers: "If you'll come up one by one, unarmed, I'll engage to
clap you all in irons... You can't find the treasure. You can't sail the
ship... Your ship's in irons, Master Silver; you're on a lee shore." Flies
the colours under fire: "Strike my colours!... No, sir, not I." Wounded in
the attack (shoulder-blade and calf). Last word to Silver: "Ah!"

### Long John Silver ("Barbecue," "the sea-cook")
"His left leg was cut off close by the hip, and under the left shoulder he
carried a crutch, which he managed with wonderful dexterity, hopping about
upon it like a bird. He was very tall and strong, with a face as big as a
ham — plain and pale, but intelligent and smiling." Flint's quartermaster.
Carries the crutch by a lanyard aboard ship; the rigged hand-lines are
"Long John's earrings." Israel: "He's no common man, Barbecue... and brave —
a lion's nothing alongside of Long John!" Flint himself "was feared of me."
Verbal tics: "you may lay to that," "shiver my timbers," "by the powers,"
"dooty is dooty," "smart as paint," addresses everyone as mate/matey/son.
- Flattery: "You're a lad, you are, but you're as smart as paint." (He uses
  the identical phrase on Dick in the barrel — the tell that damns him.)
- Barrel speech: "I'm fifty, mark you; once back from this cruise, I set up
  gentleman in earnest." "Wait is what I say; but when the time comes, why,
  let her rip!" "I give my vote — death... I claim Trelawney. I'll wring his
  calf's head off his body with these hands."
- Killing Tom: whips the crutch "hurtling through the air... right between
  the shoulders," then knifes him twice. "Agile as a monkey even without
  leg or crutch."
- Embassy: "Them that die'll be the lucky ones." (After spitting into the
  spring.)
- Facing down the crew: "There's never a man looked me between the eyes and
  seen a good day a'terwards, Tom Morgan, you may lay to that." "Take a
  cutlass, him that dares, and I'll see the colour of his inside, crutch
  and all, before that pipe's empty."
- Turning his coat: "you're within half a plank of death, and, what's a
  long sight worse, of torture... You save your witness, and he'll save
  your neck!"
- On the black spot cut from a Bible: "You've gone and cut this out of a
  Bible. What fool's cut a Bible?... It don't bind no more'n a ballad-book."
- At the empty cache, cool as ever: "Dig away, boys; you'll find some
  pig-nuts and I shouldn't wonder." Shoots George Merry: "George, I reckon
  I settled you."
- End: slips away at the Spanish-American port with "one of the sacks of
  coin, worth perhaps three or four hundred guineas." "I think we were all
  pleased to be so cheaply quit of him."

### Captain Flint (the parrot)
Green, maybe "two hundred years old," swears "passing belief for
wickedness," quieted by a handkerchief over the cage. Named for the dead
pirate. Screams "Pieces of eight! Pieces of eight! Pieces of eight!... like
the clacking of a tiny mill" and "Stand by to go about!" She betrays Jim's
midnight return to the blockhouse — "keeping better watch than any human
being." History per Silver: sailed with Cap'n England, was at Madagascar,
Malabar, Surinam, Providence, Portobello, "at the fishing up of the wrecked
plate ships. It's there she learned 'Pieces of eight,' and little wonder;
three hundred and fifty thousand of 'em, Hawkins!"

### Israel Hands
Coxswain, "Flint's gunner," "a careful, wily, old, experienced seaman."
- "I want to go into that cabin, I do. I want their pickles and wines, and
  that."
- Creed: "I never seen good come o' goodness yet. Him as strikes first is
  my fancy; dead men don't bite; them's my views — amen, so be it."
- Wounded, plays weak, hides a dirk, gulls Jim below for "a bottle of wine
  — this here brandy's too strong for my head."
- On O'Brien's corpse: "s'pose you was to heave him overboard. I ain't
  partic'lar as a rule... but I don't reckon him ornamental now, do you?"
- Theology: "do you take it as a dead man is dead for good, or do he come
  alive again?... I'll chance it with the sperrits, Jim."
- Death: pinned Jim to the mast with a thrown dirk; Jim's pistols "went off"
  and Hands "plunged head first into the water... being both shot and
  drowned."

### Ben Gunn
Marooned three years. "his voice sounded hoarse and awkward, like a rusty
lock." Ragged patchwork of "old ship's canvas and old sea-cloth" held by
"brass buttons, bits of stick, and loops of tarry gaskin." Childlike,
cunning, pious-relapsed, obsessed with cheese:
- "I'm poor Ben Gunn, I am; and I haven't spoke with a Christian these three
  years."
- "Marooned three years agone... and lived on goats since then, and berries,
  and oysters... You mightn't happen to have a piece of cheese about you,
  now? No? Well, many's the long night I've dreamed of cheese — toasted,
  mostly — and woke up again, and here I were."
- "I'm rich! Rich! I says... you'll bless your stars, you will, you was the
  first that found me!"
- "Not a man — with one — leg?" (his terror of Silver).
- Wants "one thousand pounds out of money that's as good as a man's own
  already" AND a passage home; message formula: "a precious sight more
  confidence" + "Ben Gunn has reasons of his own" — "and then you'll give
  him a nip, like I do."
- His boat: "there's my boat, that I made with my two hands. I keep her
  under the white rock."
- It was Ben who found the skeleton, rifled it, dug up the treasure ("it
  was the haft of his pick-axe that lay broken in the excavation") and
  carried it to his cave "two months before the arrival of the HISPANIOLA."
- Plays Flint's ghost: sings "Fifteen Men," then wails "Darby M'Graw! Darby
  M'Graw!... Fetch aft the rum, Darby!" — Flint's actual last words.
- Ending: gets a thousand pounds, spends it "in nineteen days."

### The mutineers
- **Job Anderson**, boatswain, leads the stockade assault, "At 'em, all
  hands — all hands!"; cut down by Gray.
- **George Merry**, "a long, ill-looking, yellow-eyed man of five and
  thirty," ringleader of the black-spot council, "eyes the colour of lemon
  peel" with fever; shot at the cache.
- **Tom Morgan**, "old, grey-haired, mahogany-faced sailor," superstitious:
  "No good'll come o' that, I said" (the cut Bible).
- **Dick Johnson**, youngest hand, corrupted in the barrel; owns the Bible;
  falls to fever. "He's seen his slice of luck, has Dick."
- **O'Brien**, red night-cap, "a rank Irelander," killed by Hands.
- **Tom** and **Alan**, the honest hands murdered ashore; Tom's stand:
  "If I die like a dog, I'll die in my dooty."
- **Abraham Gray**, carpenter's mate, honest; answers Smollett's 30-second
  call, fights well, ends "mate and part owner of a fine full-rigged ship."
- **Mr. Arrow**, drunken mate, lost overboard before landfall.
- Faithful servants: **Redruth** (dies at the stockade: "I wish I had had a
  lick at them with the gun first"), **Hunter** (dies of the attack),
  **Joyce** ("shot through the head, never to move again").

### Captain Flint (the dead pirate, offstage)
"The bloodthirstiest buccaneer that sailed." Died at Savannah crying for
rum; last words "Darby M'Graw... fetch aft the rum." Blue in the face.
Buried the treasure with six men and killed them all; laid Allardyce as a
compass pointer. Gave Billy the map "at Savannah, when he lay a-dying."
"Flint his own self was feared of me" (Silver). Six hundred... "There's
seven hundred thousand pound not a quarter of a mile from here."

## 3. Object inventory

- **The black spot** (twice). #1: "a little round of paper, blackened on
  the one side... on the other side... this short message: 'You have till
  ten tonight.'" #2 (to Silver): cut from Dick's Bible, "about the size of a
  crown piece," printed side a verse of Revelation — "Without are dogs and
  murderers" — the blank side lettered "Depposed" in wood ash.
- **Billy's sea-chest**: initial "B" burned on top. Contents in order: "a
  suit of very good clothes, carefully brushed and folded... a quadrant, a
  tin canikin, several sticks of tobacco, two brace of very handsome
  pistols, a piece of bar silver, an old Spanish watch... a pair of
  compasses mounted with brass, and five or six curious West Indian shells
  ... an old boat-cloak, whitened with sea-salt" — and beneath it all "a
  bundle tied up in oilcloth, and looking like papers, and a canvas bag that
  gave forth, at a touch, the jingle of gold."
- **The oilskin packet**: sewn shut; contains Billy's account book (crosses
  for ships sunk, "Offe Caraccas," "Bones, his pile") and the sealed **map**.
- **The treasure map**: "the map of an island, with latitude and longitude,
  soundings, names of hills and bays and inlets... nine miles long and five
  across, shaped... like a fat dragon standing up... a hill in the centre
  part marked 'The Spy-glass'... three crosses of red ink — two on the north
  part of the island, one in the southwest — and beside this last... 'Bulk
  of treasure here.'" Back of the map, in J.F.'s hand:
      Tall tree, Spy-glass shoulder, bearing a point to the N. of N.N.E.
      Skeleton Island E.S.E. and by E.
      Ten feet.
      The bar silver is in the north cache; you can find it by the trend of
      the east hummock, ten fathoms south of the black crag with the face on
      it.
      The arms are easy found, in the sand-hill, N. point of north inlet
      cape, bearing E. and a quarter N.
      J.F.
- **The key** on tarry string round Billy's neck; **his gully** (clasp
  knife) with the crooked handle; **pocket compass**; **tinder box**;
  **brass telescope**; **cutlass**.
- **Mrs. Crossley's bag** (mother counts out only her due — guineas,
  doubloons, louis d'ors, pieces of eight).
- **The loaded pistol** the hamlet lends Jim.
- **The squire's note** to John Silver at the Spy-glass.
- **The apple barrel** "standing broached in the waist" — scarce an apple
  left; Jim climbs in bodily.
- **Long Tom**, the long brass nine (ship's swivel gun); round-shot;
  muskets, cutlasses, pistols, powder-horns throughout.
- **The Jolly Roger** Jim strikes: "handed down their cursed black flag, and
  chucked it overboard. 'God save the king!' said I."
- **The British colours** Smollett flies at the log-house and spreads over
  Redruth's body.
- **The coracle**: "home-made if ever anything was home-made; a rude,
  lop-sided framework of tough wood, and stretched upon that a covering of
  goat-skin, with the hair inside... exceedingly light and portable," hidden
  under a goatskin tent below the white rock. "Queer to handle till you
  knew her way."
- **The hawser**: "taut as a bowstring... a taut hawser, suddenly cut, is a
  thing as dangerous as a kicking horse" — Jim waits for a puff of wind to
  slacken it, then cuts strand by strand.
- **Hands' dirk**: "a long knife, or rather a short dirk, discoloured to
  the hilt with blood," hidden in his jacket, finally thrown to pin Jim's
  shoulder to the mast.
- **Brandy bottle / wine bottle**; biscuit, pickled fruits, raisins, cheese
  from the cabin stores; the water-breaker.
- **The doctor's snuff-box of Parmesan** ("that's for Ben Gunn!").
- **Picks and shovels** — "the very first necessary they brought ashore."
- **The skeleton** (Allardyce): "the man lay perfectly straight — his feet
  pointing in one direction, his hands, raised above his head like a
  diver's, pointing directly in the opposite" — a compass pointer reading
  "E.S.E. and by E." Rifled: "not a copper doit nor a baccy box."
- **The cache**: broken pick-shaft, packing-case boards branded WALRUS, one
  two-guinea piece in the earth.
- **The treasure** in Ben's cave: "great heaps of coin and quadrilaterals
  built of bars of gold... English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Georges,
  and Louises, doubloons and double guineas and moidores and sequins...
  nearly every variety of money in the world," ~700,000 pounds. Packed out
  in bread-bags.
- **The bar silver** — never recovered: "The bar silver and the arms still
  lie, for all that I know, where Flint buried them; and certainly they
  shall lie there for me."

## 4. Timeline of major beats (chapter by chapter, brief)

PART ONE — The Old Buccaneer
1. Billy Bones lodges at the Benbow; pays Jim to watch for a one-legged
   seafaring man; sings "Fifteen men"; cowed once by Dr. Livesey.
2. Black Dog visits; cutlass fight; Billy has a stroke; Livesey bleeds him.
3. Billy begs rum, tells Jim of Flint and the black spot; Jim's father dies;
   Blind Pew comes and presses the spot into Billy's palm ("You have till
   ten tonight"); Billy drops dead.
4. Jim and mother refused help at the hamlet; return; take the key from the
   body; open the chest; mother counts her due; whistle on the hill; they
   flee with the oilskin packet; hide under the bridge.
5. Pew's crew ransacks the inn; "Flint's fist" missing; quarrel; revenue
   riders arrive; Pew trampled dead; lugger escapes from Kitt's Hole.
6. At the hall: packet opened — account book and map. Trelawney: "I fit out
   a ship in Bristol dock." Livesey: "There's only one man I'm afraid of...
   You; for you cannot hold your tongue."

PART TWO — The Sea-cook
7. Weeks pass; Trelawney's letter: the Hispaniola bought; cook engaged —
   "Long John Silver... has lost a leg." Jim says goodbye to his mother.
8. Jim delivers a note to the Spy-glass; spots Black Dog there; Silver's
   masterly performance disarms all suspicion.
9. Smollett's misgivings; powder and berths shifted aft.
10. The voyage; Arrow lost overboard; Silver's galley and the parrot; the
    apple barrel broached in the waist.
11. THE APPLE BARREL: Jim overhears Silver corrupting Dick, Hands pressing
    for action, plan to murder the cabin party after the treasure is aboard.
    "Land ho!"
12. Council of war in the cabin: seven true men against nineteen.
13. Anchored in Kidd's Anchorage; gloom and fever-smell; Silver manages the
    restless crew; Jim slips ashore with the landing party.

PART THREE — My Shore Adventure
14. Jim spies Silver's recruitment of Tom in the dell; Alan's death-scream
    across the marsh; Silver kills Tom with crutch and knife.
15. The man of the island: Ben Gunn — cheese, marooning, "I'm rich," his
    boat under the white rock; cannon-fire begins.

PART FOUR — The Stockade (doctor narrates 16–18)
16. The faithful abandon ship by jolly-boat, provision the log-house.
17. Last trip: overloaded, current; Long Tom fired; boat sinks in the
    shallows; stores lost.
18. Skirmish through the woods; Redruth shot dead; colours run up over the
    log-house; cannonade.
19. (Jim resumes) Jim reaches the stockade; garrison life; Livesey plans to
    parley with Ben Gunn.
20. Silver's embassy under a flag of truce; terms refused. "Them that
    die'll be the lucky ones."
21. THE ATTACK: boarders over the palisade; melee at the loopholes; Anderson
    killed; Joyce and Hunter dead, Smollett wounded; five pirates down.

PART FIVE — My Sea Adventure
22. Jim takes French leave with biscuit and pistols; finds the coracle
    under the white rock.
23. Night: cuts the Hispaniola's hawser strand by strand between puffs of
    wind; glimpses Hands and O'Brien fighting; swept to sea in the ebb.
24. Dawn cruise of the coracle down the west coast; sea lions; sights the
    derelict-sailing Hispaniola.
25. Boards her by the jib-boom; coracle smashed; strikes the Jolly Roger;
    bargains with the wounded Hands.
26. ISRAEL HANDS: the wine errand and the hidden dirk; beaching in North
    Inlet; the chase; Jim in the cross-trees re-primes his pistols; the
    thrown dirk pins his shoulder; Hands shot, falls into the bay.
27. O'Brien overboard; ship secured; night march across the island; the
    blockhouse in darkness — "Pieces of eight!" — captured by the pirates.

PART SIX — Captain Silver
28. In the enemy's camp: Silver, deposed-in-waiting, shields Jim; Jim's
    defiance speech; "First and last, we've split upon Jim Hawkins!"
29. The black spot again ("Depposed," cut from Dick's Bible); Silver turns
    the council with the chart the doctor gave him.
30. On parole: Livesey treats the sick pirates; Jim refuses to escape
    because he passed his word; reveals where the ship lies; "look out for
    squalls when you find it."
31. Treasure hunt: Jim led on a rope like a dancing bear; the skeleton
    pointer, E.S.E. and by E.
32. The voice among the trees: "Fifteen men," then "Darby M'Graw!" — panic,
    then Merry recognizes Ben Gunn ("dead or alive, nobody minds him").
33. THE EMPTY CACHE. Merry's mutiny-in-miniature; three musket-shots from
    the thicket; Merry and the bandaged man killed; Silver: "George, I
    reckon I settled you." The story of Ben's dig; to the cave; the gold.
34. Loading the treasure; the three maroons left ashore (with stores);
    Silver escapes at a Spanish-American port with a sack of coin;
    homecoming; fates of all; "Pieces of eight!" nightmare close.

## 5. Iconic scenes any player will expect

1. **The black spot at the Benbow** — tapping stick, the grip like a vise,
   "You have till ten tonight," Billy dead on the parlour floor.
2. **The sea-chest heist** — key round the dead man's neck, layers of the
   chest, the blind man's stick tapping outside, whistle on the hill, flight
   with the oilskin packet, hiding under the bridge, Pew trampled.
3. **Opening the map at the hall** — "I fit out a ship in Bristol dock."
4. **The Spy-glass tavern** — Black Dog bolting, Silver's charm offensive.
5. **The apple barrel** — a dozen words in the dark and the whole plot
   plain; "smart as paint" reused on Dick; "Land ho!" as release.
6. **Silver kills Tom** — the thrown crutch, the pattern of his menace.
7. **"The man of the island"** — Ben Gunn, cheese, "I'm rich."
8. **The stockade siege** — flag kept flying, the boarding, cutlasses.
9. **Silver's embassy** — "Them that die'll be the lucky ones."
10. **Cutting the hawser / cruise of the coracle** — waiting for the wind,
    riding the swells lying flat.
11. **Jim vs Israel Hands** — the wine trick, the dirk, the cross-trees,
    "One more step, Mr. Hands, and I'll blow your brains out! Dead men
    don't bite, you know."
12. **"Pieces of eight!" in the dark blockhouse** — the parrot as sentry.
13. **The black spot on a Bible page** — "Depposed."
14. **The skeleton pointer** and **Flint's voice among the trees**.
15. **The empty cache** — "Dig away, boys; you'll find some pig-nuts."
16. **The cave of gold** and **Silver's ambiguous escape**.

## 7. Notes for adaptation (observations made while reading)

- The book's engine of tension is *overheard knowledge*: the apple barrel,
  the dell, the loophole, the cross-trees. Listening and hiding are the
  verbs of the novel — cheap to model in a parser and inherently turn-based.
- Jim is always exactly where the plot needs him via disobedience. The game
  should reward wandering off (both "follies" are the player's best moves).
- Stevenson pre-gamified the map: real bearings, a decoy cross, a pointer
  puzzle (the skeleton), and an anticlimax cache with the true prize
  elsewhere. The bar-silver note on the map's back is an unexploited thread
  ("still lie ... where Flint buried them") — perfect optional treasure.
- The narrative hands the middle section (chs. 16–18) to the doctor while
  Jim is elsewhere; a game can simply keep the camera on Jim and compress
  those events into arrival-at-the-stockade exposition.
- Silver switches sides five times and is never punished; his charm is the
  point. In game terms he is a scene-state machine, not a free-roaming NPC.
- The parrot is a portable jump-scare and comic relief; the book itself
  uses her as an alarm system (the blockhouse capture).
- Chronology conveniently splits into three self-contained stages with
  one-way transitions (coast -> Bristol/voyage -> island), which maps
  cleanly onto gated acts; nothing from Act I is needed later except the
  map and Billy's gully knife (which the book itself has Jim reuse to cut
  the key-string and later the hawser is cut with "my sea-gully").
