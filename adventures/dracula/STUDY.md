# DRACULA — Study Notes for the zorkllm Adaptation

Source: Project Gutenberg #345 (book.txt in this directory), Bram Stoker, 1897.
Read in full 2026-08-09. Line numbers below refer to book.txt.

The novel is epistolary: every chapter is somebody's journal, letter, log,
telegram, or newspaper cutting. This is a gift to a text adventure: "From the
journal of Jonathan Harker" is a natural scene header, a natural save-point
framing, and a natural excuse for switching the player character.

---

## PART 1: CASTLE DRACULA (Chapters I-IV, Jonathan Harker's Journal)

### 1.1 The journey (Ch. I, lines 116-620)

Route: Munich -> Vienna -> Buda-Pesth -> Klausenburgh -> **Bistritz** (Golden
Krone Hotel) -> coach toward Bukovina -> **Borgo Pass** at midnight ->
Dracula's caleche -> Castle Dracula. Date: 3-5 May. It is the eve of St.
George's Day, "when the clock strikes midnight, all the evil things in the
world will have full sway."

Key beats and quotes (all verbatim):

- The landlady begs Harker not to go, then: "she put the rosary round my
  neck, and said, 'For your mother's sake,' and went out of the room."
  **This is where the player gets the crucifix.** Harker, an English
  Churchman, thinks it "idolatrous" but keeps it.
- The crowd at the coach: "'Ordog'--Satan, 'pokol'--hell, 'stregoica'--witch,
  'vrolok' and 'vlkoslak'--both of which mean the same thing... either
  were-wolf or vampire." They cross themselves and point two fingers at him
  (charm against the evil eye).
- Gifts pressed on him in the coach: crucifix, garlic, wild rose, mountain
  ash (he lists them at line 1161: "What meant the giving of the crucifix,
  of the garlic, of the wild rose, of the mountain ash?").
- The caleche driver (Dracula himself, disguised): "a tall man, with a long
  brown beard and a great black hat"; "the gleam of a pair of very bright
  eyes, which seemed red in the lamplight"; his hand "caught my arm in a
  grip of steel."
- A passenger whispers Burger's "Lenore": "'Denn die Todten reiten
  schnell'--('For the dead travel fast.')"
- The blue flames: "a faint flickering blue flame" over buried treasure,
  seen only this one night of the year; the driver piles stones to mark the
  spots; when he stands between Harker and the flame he does not block it
  ("he did not obstruct it, for I could see its ghostly flicker all the
  same").
- The ring of wolves: "a ring of wolves, with white teeth and lolling red
  tongues"; the driver "swept his long arms, as though brushing aside some
  impalpable obstacle, and the wolves fell back."
- Arrival: "the courtyard of a vast ruined castle, from whose tall black
  windows came no ray of light, and whose broken battlements showed a jagged
  line against the moonlit sky."

### 1.2 The castle, room by room (Ch. II-IV)

Rooms actually visited or seen in the text:

1. **Courtyard** — "of considerable size... several dark ways led from it
   under great round arches." The caleche disappears down one of the dark
   openings. Later the Szgany (gypsies) camp here; the boxes are piled here;
   a grieving mother is devoured by wolves here ("Monster, give me my
   child!").
2. **Great door / entrance hall** — "a great door, old and studded with
   large iron nails, set in a projecting doorway of massive stone." No bell
   or knocker. Opens with "rattling chains and the clanking of massive
   bolts... a key turned with the loud grating noise of long disuse."
   Harker later finds he can undo bolts and chains but THE DOOR IS LOCKED
   AND THE KEY IS GONE — the escape problem of Act I. Off the hall: one or
   two small open rooms with "old furniture, dusty with age and moth-eaten."
3. **Winding stair and great passage** — "up a great winding stair, and
   along another great passage, on whose stone floor our steps rang heavily."
4. **Dining room** — "a well-lit room in which a table was spread for
   supper, and on whose mighty hearth a great fire of logs... flamed and
   flared." Gold table service. Dracula's welcome happens at the entrance:
   "Welcome to my house! Enter freely and of your own will!" and "I am
   Dracula; and I bid you welcome, Mr. Harker, to my house." Later: "Listen
   to them--the children of the night. What music they make!"
5. **Octagonal room** — "a small octagonal room lit by a single lamp, and
   seemingly without a window of any sort." Anteroom between dining room and
   bedroom.
6. **Harker's bedroom** — great bedroom, log fire, curtained bed, no mirror
   anywhere ("There is not even a toilet glass on my table"). Window looks
   into the courtyard, later described as barred. The shaving scene happens
   here: Harker hangs his little shaving glass by the window; Dracula has
   NO REFLECTION; Harker nicks his chin; "his eyes blazed with a sort of
   demoniac fury, and he suddenly made a grab at my throat... his hand
   touched the string of beads which held the crucifix. It made an instant
   change in him." Dracula: "Take care how you cut yourself. It is more
   dangerous than you think in this country." He flings the mirror out the
   window: "It is a foul bauble of man's vanity. Away with it!"
7. **Library/study** — off the dining room; "a vast number of English
   books... history, geography, politics, political economy, botany,
   geology, law," an atlas that falls open at England with rings marked at
   Purfleet (Carfax), Exeter, and Whitby; Bradshaw's railway Guide. Most of
   the Dracula conversations happen here. He commands: "You may go anywhere
   you wish in the castle, except where the doors are locked, where of
   course you will not wish to go."
8. **South-facing room / stair window** — "a room looking towards the
   South... The castle is on the very edge of a terrible precipice. A stone
   falling from the window would fall a thousand feet without touching
   anything!" From this window Harker twice watches Dracula crawl out his
   own window "face down... with his cloak spreading out around him like
   great wings... just as a lizard moves along a wall."
9. **Locked doors everywhere** — "doors, doors, doors everywhere, and all
   locked and bolted. In no place save from the windows in the castle walls
   is there an available exit. The castle is a veritable prison, and I am a
   prisoner!"
10. **The ladies' wing (the brides' room)** — reached by a door at the top
    of a stairway that is stuck, not locked ("the hinges had fallen
    somewhat, and the heavy door rested on the floor" — forcing it open is
    a classic puzzle). "A wing of the castle further to the right... and a
    storey lower down"; windows west and south over the precipice; old
    comfortable furniture, deep dust, moonlight through diamond panes, a
    little oak table. HERE THE THREE BRIDES APPEAR if you linger at night:
    two dark, one fair; "brilliant white teeth that shone like pearls
    against the ruby of their voluptuous lips"; they cast no shadow in the
    moonlight. "Go on! You are first, and we shall follow; yours is the
    right to begin." / "He is young and strong; there are kisses for us
    all." Dracula's intervention: "How dare you touch him, any of you?...
    This man belongs to me!" The brides: "You yourself never loved; you
    never love!" Dracula's promise: "When I am done with him you shall kiss
    him at your will." They take a bag with "a half-smothered child" in it.
    They fade "into the rays of the moonlight and pass out through the
    window."
11. **Dracula's room** — always locked from the hall; reachable ONLY by
    climbing out a window and crawling the wall to his window (Harker does
    this twice: "Where his body has gone why may not another body go?").
    Inside: barely furnished, dusty, and "a great heap of gold in one
    corner--gold of all kinds, Roman, and British, and Austrian, and
    Hungarian, and Greek and Turkish money... none of it less than three
    hundred years old," plus jewelled chains and ornaments. No key found.
    A heavy door in the corner leads down.
12. **Circular stairway down + tunnel passage** — "a stone passage to a
    circular stairway, which went steeply down... at the bottom a dark,
    tunnel-like passage, through which came a deathly, sickly odour, the
    odour of old earth newly turned."
13. **The ruined chapel** — "an old, ruined chapel, which had evidently been
    used as a graveyard. The roof was broken, and in two places were steps
    leading to vaults... the ground had recently been dug over, and the
    earth placed in great wooden boxes" — the FIFTY boxes. No other outlet.
14. **The vaults** — three explorable; two hold "fragments of old coffins
    and piles of dust"; in the third, in one of the great boxes on new-dug
    earth, LIES THE COUNT: "either dead or asleep... the eyes were open and
    stony, but without the glassiness of death... no pulse, no breath, no
    beating of the heart." Second visit (30 June): "looking as if his youth
    had been half renewed... on the lips were gouts of fresh blood... He lay
    like a filthy leech, exhausted with his repletion." Harker strikes with
    a workman's shovel; the head turns, the basilisk eyes paralyse him, and
    the blow only gashes the forehead; the lid falls shut.

### 1.3 The plot mechanics of Act I (all gameable)

- Dracula is nocturnal; conversations always end at cockcrow ("Why, there
  is the morning again!"). Harker: "It has always been at night-time that I
  have been molested or threatened... I have not yet seen the Count in the
  daylight. Can it be that he sleeps when others wake?"
- Dracula does all the housework secretly (no servants): seen making the
  bed and laying the table through a door-chink — proof Harker is alone.
- The three post-dated letters (June 12 / 19 / 29): "I know now the span of
  my life. God help me!" A built-in countdown clock.
- The Szgany camp in the courtyard; Harker throws letters through his
  window bars with a gold piece; the Szgany hand them straight to Dracula,
  who burns the shorthand one: "a vile thing, an outrage upon friendship
  and hospitality!"
- Harker's own clothes and papers are stolen; Dracula wears them on his
  night raids (baby-stealing blamed on "the Englishman").
- The mother at the gate: "Monster, give me my child!" — wolves devour her.
- The Slovaks deliver the 50 great square boxes with rope handles; later
  the Szgany fill them with chapel earth ("a far-away muffled sound as of
  mattock and spade").
- The dust motes in the moonlight materialize into the three brides —
  sleeping anywhere outside the safe rooms is lethal ("Should sleep now or
  ever overcome you... haste to your own chamber").
- Final trap: door bolted, brides told "To-night is mine. To-morrow night
  is yours!" Harker's last plan: scale the wall to the sheer face and climb
  down with some of the gold: "I shall take some of the gold with me, lest
  I want it later... the precipice is steep and high. At its foot a man may
  sleep--as a man." (He escapes; we learn in Ch. VIII/XXV he reached
  Buda-Pesth with brain fever, nursed by nuns at the Hospital of St. Joseph
  and Ste. Mary, where Mina marries him.)

### 1.4 Dracula's voice (reusable lines)

- "Welcome to my house! Enter freely and of your own will!"
- "Come freely. Go safely; and leave something of the happiness you bring!"
- "Listen to them--the children of the night. What music they make!"
- "We Szekelys have a right to be proud, for in our veins flows the blood
  of many brave races who fought as the lion fights, for lordship."
- "Here I am noble; I am boyar; the common people know me, and I am master."
- "I love the shade and the shadow, and would be alone with my thoughts
  when I may."
- "Take care how you cut yourself. It is more dangerous than you think in
  this country."
- "You may go anywhere you wish in the castle, except where the doors are
  locked, where of course you will not wish to go."
- "Welcome the coming; speed the parting guest."
- "Your time is not yet come. Wait! Have patience! To-night is mine.
  To-morrow night is yours!"
- Courtly menace formula: elaborate old-world politeness + "my friend" +
  the mask slipping for one sentence, then restored.

## PART 2: WHITBY, THE DEMETER, LUCY'S FIRST WOUNDS (Chapters V-IX)

### 2.1 Whitby geography (Mina's Journal, 24 July onward)

- The river Esk "runs through a deep valley, which broadens out as it comes
  near the harbour"; a great viaduct crosses it. The old town is "all
  red-roofed, and seem piled up one over the other anyhow, like the pictures
  we see of Nuremberg." The Westenras lodge at the Crescent (Mina and Lucy
  share a lockable bedroom; Mina later ties the key to her wrist).
- **The harbour**: "one long granite wall stretching out into the sea, with
  a curve outwards at the end of it, in the middle of which is a
  lighthouse." Narrow opening between two lighthoused piers. Outside runs
  "a great reef" with "a buoy with a bell, which swings in bad weather, and
  sends in a mournful sound on the wind." A searchlight sits on the East
  Cliff. The Drawbridge is the ONLY way from West Cliff to East Cliff.
- **The abbey**: "Right over the town is the ruin of Whitby Abbey, which was
  sacked by the Danes... a most noble ruin, of immense size... there is a
  legend that a white lady is seen in one of the windows."
- **St. Mary's churchyard, East Cliff**: "a big graveyard, all full of
  tombstones... it lies right over the town, and has a full view of the
  harbour... part of the bank has fallen away, and some of the graves have
  been destroyed." Flat tombstones = "thruff-steans." Walks with seats;
  old sailors sit there all day.
- **The 199 steps**: "They lead from the town up to the church... and they
  wind up in a delicate curve; the slope is so gentle that a horse could
  easily walk up and down them."
- **The suicide's grave / the seat**: Lucy's favourite seat rests on the
  slab of George Canon, suicide ("blew nigh the top of his head off with an
  old musket"), epitaph "Sacred to the memory of George Canon, who died, in
  the hope of a glorious resurrection... falling from the rocks at
  Kettleness." Swales claims half the graves are empty.
- **Tate Hill Pier**: sand-and-gravel corner under the East Cliff where the
  Demeter grounds; the churchyard hangs directly above it.
- Pre-storm atmosphere (6 Aug): "Everything is grey--except the green grass,
  which seems like emerald amongst it... grey clouds... hang over the grey
  sea, into which the sand-points stretch like grey fingers"; "there is a
  'brool' over the sea that sounds like some presage of doom."

### 2.2 The Demeter (Ch. VII — Dailygraph cutting + captain's log)

Russian schooner from Varna, ballast of silver sand, cargo "a number of
great wooden boxes filled with mould" — the 50 boxes, consigned to
S. F. Billington, 7 The Crescent, Whitby. Log staging (compressible into a
readable in-game document):
- 6 July sail; 16 July first man vanishes; 17 July Olgaren sees "a tall,
  thin man, who was not like any of the crew"; full stem-to-stern search
  finds nothing; men vanish one by one through July.
- 3 Aug: the mate, mad, stabs It — "I crept behind It, and gave It my
  knife; but the knife went through It, empty as the air" — then jumps
  overboard: "The sea will save me from Him, and it is all that is left!"
- 4 Aug: the captain, alone: "I shall tie my hands to the wheel when my
  strength begins to fail, and along with them I shall tie that which
  He--It!--dare not touch" (his crucifix and beads).
- Arrival 8 Aug in the great storm: the schooner, all sail set, "leaping
  from wave to wave," grounds on Tate Hill Pier; "lashed to the helm was a
  corpse, with drooping head, which swung horribly to and fro"; "the very
  instant the shore was touched, an immense dog sprang up on deck from
  below... and running forward, jumped from the bow on the sand," vanishing
  toward the East Cliff. The log addendum is found corked in a bottle in
  the dead captain's pocket. Next day a mastiff is found dead, "its throat
  was torn away."
- The captain gets a hero's funeral, carried up the 199 steps, buried near
  Lucy's seat. Swales is found dead on the seat the same day, neck broken,
  "a look of fear and horror on his face."

### 2.3 Lucy's first wounds (the churchyard scene, 11 Aug)

- Mina wakes; Lucy's bed empty; from the West Cliff she sees across the
  harbour: "on our favourite seat, the silver light of the moon struck a
  half-reclining figure, snowy white... something dark stood behind the
  seat where the white figure shone, and bent over it." Closer: "something
  raised a head, and from where I was I could see a white face and red,
  gleaming eyes."
- The rescue kit: Mina throws her big heavy shawl over Lucy and "fastened
  the shawl at her throat with a big safety-pin; but I must have been
  clumsy... and pinched or pricked her with it." Next day: "two little red
  points like pin-pricks, and on the band of her nightdress was a drop of
  blood." Mina puts her own shoes on Lucy and daubs mud on her own feet.
- Escalation: the great bat in whirling circles outside the window; "His
  red eyes again! They are just the same" at sunset on the seat; Lucy
  asleep at the sill with "something that looked like a good-sized bird"
  beside her; the wounds "still open, and, if anything, larger than
  before... like little white dots with red centres."
- Lucy's dream-memory of being fed on (reusable as trance narration):
  "something long and dark with red eyes... and something very sweet and
  very bitter all around me at once; and then I seemed sinking into deep
  green water, and there was a singing in my ears... my soul seemed to go
  out from my body and float about the air."

### 2.4 Renfield's introduction (Seward's phonograph diary)

- "R. M. Renfield, aetat 59.--Sanguine temperament; great physical
  strength; morbidly excitable." Seward's coinage: "a zoophagous
  (life-eating) maniac; what he desires is to absorb as many lives as he
  can." The ladder: flies -> spiders fed on flies -> sparrows fed on
  spiders -> begs for a kitten ("that I can play with, and teach, and
  feed--and feed--and feed!") -> denied, eats the birds raw.
- His notebook: "whole pages of it are filled with masses of figures...
  single numbers added up in batches, and then the totals added in batches
  again," — he is bookkeeping consumed lives.
- Master-worship (at Dracula's arrival at Carfax, 19 Aug): "I don't want to
  talk to you: you don't count now; the Master is at hand." At the Carfax
  chapel door: "I am here to do Your bidding, Master. I am Your slave, and
  You will reward me, for I shall be faithful." Later: "All over! all over!
  He has deserted me. No hope for me now unless I do it for myself!"
- Renfield escapes twice to press against "the old ironbound oak door of
  the chapel" at Carfax. His room's window can be forced; the asylum
  grounds adjoin Carfax over "the high wall which separates our grounds
  from those of the deserted house."

### 2.5 Voices (Ch. V-IX)

- Lucy: "Just fancy! THREE proposals in one day! Isn't it awful!"; "Why
  can't they let a girl marry three men, or as many as want her?"
- Quincey Morris: "Won't you just hitch up alongside of me and let us go
  down the long road together, driving in double harness?"; "I believe you
  clean grit, right through to the very depths of your soul."
- Swales (Whitby dialect): "It be all fool-talk, lock, stock, and barrel";
  death-premonition: "There's something in that wind and in the hoast
  beyont that sounds, and looks, and tastes, and smells like death. It's in
  the air; I feel it comin'."
- Van Helsing's first appearance (letter): "When I have received your
  letter I am already coming to you... for I come to my friend when he call
  me to aid those he holds dear." Diagnosis: "With you I agree that there
  has been much blood lost; it has been, but is not."; "This is no jest,
  but life and death, perhaps more."
- Seward on chasing Renfield: "Chasing an errant swarm of bees is nothing
  to following a naked lunatic, when the fit of escaping is upon him!"
- Note: "The blood is the life!" occurs later than Ch. IX (see Part 3).

### 2.6 The paper trail of the boxes (investigation seed)

Billington ships the fifty boxes by Great Northern Railway (Whitby 9:30 pm,
King's Cross 4:30); Carter, Paterson & Co. cart them to Carfax and stack
them "in the partially ruined building... the ancient chapel of the
mansion." The labelled keys are left "in parcel in main hall"; the
proprietor holds a duplicate. Invoice reads "fifty cases of common earth,
to be used for experimental purposes."

## PART 3: LUCY'S DECLINE, DEATH, AND STAKING (Chapters X-XVI)

### 3.1 The defense mechanics (verbatim rules — these become puzzles)

- **Garlic application procedure** (Van Helsing, 11 Sept): "First he
  fastened up the windows and latched them securely; next, taking a handful
  of the flowers, he rubbed them all over the sashes... Then with the wisp
  he rubbed all over the jamb of the door, above, below, and at each side,
  and round the fireplace in the same way." Wreath fixed round Lucy's neck:
  "Take care you do not disturb it; and even if the room feel close, do not
  to-night open the window or the door." The garlic comes from Haarlem
  ("my friend Vanderpool raise herb in his glass-houses all the year").
- **Removing the garlic = relapse**: Mrs. Westenra "took them all away and
  opened a bit of the window to let in a little fresh air." VH: "This poor
  mother, all unknowing... does such thing as lose her daughter body and
  soul."
- **Sleep tug-of-war**: "whenever she got into that lethargic state... she
  put the flowers from her; but that when she waked she clutched them
  close."
- **The bat signature**: "a great bat, which wheeled round... and every now
  and again struck the window with its wings."
- **Escalation when garlic holds** — the wolf: "in the aperture of the
  broken panes there was the head of a great, gaunt grey wolf." Then mist:
  "a whole myriad of little specks seemed to come blowing in through the
  broken window, and wheeling and circling round like the pillar of dust."
  "The lights burn blue and dim."
- **The gold crucifix**: worn inside VH's collar; laid on dead Lucy's
  mouth; STOLEN by a servant overnight ("from the woman who robbed the dead
  and the living") — the theft ruins the timing of the quiet beheading. At
  the tomb it physically repels un-dead Lucy: "Van Helsing sprang forward
  and held between them his little golden crucifix. She recoiled from it."
- **The Host as putty** (29 Sept): "he took from his bag a mass of what
  looked like thin, wafer-like biscuit... crumbled the wafer up fine and
  worked it into the mass... began to lay them into the crevices between
  the door and its setting in the tomb." — "I am closing the tomb, so that
  the Un-Dead may not enter." — "What is that which you are using?" —
  "The Host. I brought it from Amsterdam. I have an Indulgence."
- **Movement rule**: "at sundown the Un-Dead can move"; the un-dead can
  slip "through the interstice where scarce a knife-blade could have gone."

### 3.2 Lucy's decline as a staged timer (game structure)

Watch kept = safe night; ANY lapse = relapse costing one blood donor.
- Stage 1 (7 Sept): "She will die for sheer want of blood" — TRANSFUSION
  #1: ARTHUR. Punctures found "just over the external jugular vein...
  the edges were white and worn-looking."
- Stage 2 (10 Sept): a night unwatched — TRANSFUSION #2: SEWARD.
- Stage 3 (11-12 Sept): garlic defenses installed; Lucy: "I never liked
  garlic before, but to-night it is delightful! There is peace in its
  smell."
- Stage 4 (13 Sept): mother removes garlic + opens window — TRANSFUSION
  #3: VAN HELSING.
- Stage 5 (17 Sept): VH's warning telegram misdelivered ("Sent to Carfax,
  Sussex, as no county given; delivered late by twenty-two hours") — the
  unguarded night. THE WOLF ATTACK (Lucy's memorandum): wolf smashes
  window; mother tears off the wreath and dies of heart failure; maids
  drugged with laudanum-laced sherry.
- Stage 6 (18 Sept): rescue break-in (bars sawed off a kitchen window) —
  TRANSFUSION #4: QUINCEY MORRIS ("A brave man's blood is the best thing
  on this earth when a woman is in trouble"). Quincey's question: "'What
  took it out?'"
- Stage 7 (19-20 Sept): teeth "positively longer and sharper"; the wounds
  vanish; the two-voice deathbed — voluptuous: "Arthur! Oh, my love, I am
  so glad you have come! Kiss me!" (VH: "Not for your life!... not for
  your living soul and hers!"); true Lucy: "My true friend, and his! Oh,
  guard him, and give me peace!" She dies. VH: "It is only the beginning!"
- Transfusion order canon: Arthur -> Seward -> Van Helsing -> Morris.

### 3.3 The tomb and the staking (Kingstead churchyard)

- The Westenra tomb: "a lordly death-house in a lonely churchyard, away
  from teeming London." Interior: "time-discoloured stone, and
  dust-encrusted mortar, and rusty, dank iron, and tarnished brass, and
  clouded silver-plating gave back the feeble glimmer of a candle."
  Coffin = wooden lid with screws over "the casing of lead"; VH fret-saws
  the lead and bends back the flange. Key from the coffin-man.
- The proof ladder (three nights, 26-29 Sept): (1) empty coffin at
  midnight + rescued child ("We were just in time"); (2) daytime body,
  "more radiantly beautiful than ever," teeth sharper; (3) all four
  witness un-dead Lucy, "a dark-haired woman, dressed in the cerements of
  the grave," lips "crimson with fresh blood," flinging the child down,
  "growling over it as a dog growls over a bone."
- The seduction: "Come to me, Arthur. Leave these others and come to me.
  My arms are hungry for you. Come, and we can rest together. Come, my
  husband, come!" — "something diabolically sweet in her tones."
- The kit (the long leather bag "something like a cricketing bag"):
  soldering iron and plumbing solder, blue-flame oil lamp, operating
  knives, "a round wooden stake, some two and a half or three inches
  thick and about three feet long... hardened by charring in the fire...
  sharpened to a fine point," and "a heavy hammer, such as in households
  is used in the coal-cellar."
- The instruction: "Take this stake in your left hand, ready to place the
  point over the heart, and the hammer in your right... strike in God's
  name."
- The strike: "The Thing in the coffin writhed; and a hideous,
  blood-curdling screech came from the opened red lips... But Arthur never
  faltered. He looked like a figure of Thor as his untrembling arm rose
  and fell, driving deeper and deeper the mercy-bearing stake." Then: "the
  holy calm that lay like sunshine over the wasted face." Cleanup: saw off
  the stake top, cut off the head, fill mouth with garlic, solder the lead.
- VH's fallback order (his in-case-of-death note): "find this great
  Un-Dead, and cut off his head and burn his heart or drive a stake
  through it."

### 3.4 Renfield in this stretch

- The knife attack on Seward (17 Sept): "He was lying on his belly on the
  floor licking up, like a dog, the blood which had fallen from my wounded
  wrist... simply repeating over and over again: 'The blood is the life!
  The blood is the life!'"
- Attacks the carriers moving boxes out of Carfax: "I'll frustrate them!
  They shan't rob me!... I'll fight for my Lord and Master!"

### 3.5 Van Helsing voice bank (Ch. X-XVI)

- "Knowledge is stronger than memory, and we should not trust the weaker...
  We learn from failure, not from success!"
- "Devils or no devils, or all the devils at once, it matters not; we fight
  him all the same."
- "Ah, it is the fault of our science that it wants to explain all; and if
  it explain not, then it says there is nothing to explain."
- "I want you to believe... to believe in things that you cannot."
- "But King Laugh he come like the sunshine, and he ease off the strain
  again."
- "There must be transfusion of blood at once." / "This is no jest, but
  life and death, perhaps more." / "We must pass through the bitter water
  before we reach the sweet."
- The un-dead lecture: "they cannot die, but must go on age after age
  adding new victims and multiplying the evils of the world... like as the
  ripples from a stone thrown in the water." Cure: "But if she die in
  truth, then all cease; the tiny wounds of the throats disappear."
- Tics: "friend John," "Madam Mina," "Gott in Himmel!", "Mein Gott!", a
  hissing indraw of breath when alarmed.
- The Bloofer Lady press cuttings ("The Westminster Gazette", 25 Sept):
  children lured away on Hampstead Heath, "slightly torn or wounded in the
  throat."

## PART 4: THE HUNTERS, THE RULES, THE BOX HUNT, THE VIOLATION (Ch. XVII-XXI)

### 4.1 Van Helsing's vampire lecture (Ch. XVIII — the game's rulebook)

All verbatim:
- "The _nosferatu_ do not die like the bee when he sting once. He is only
  stronger; and being stronger, have yet more power to work evil."
- **Strength**: "so strong in person as twenty men."
- **Command of beasts/elements**: "he can, within his range, direct the
  elements; the storm, the fog, the thunder; he can command all the meaner
  things: the rat, and the owl, and the bat--the moth, and the fox, and
  the wolf."
- **Ageless, feeds to grow young**: "he can even grow younger... when his
  special pabulum is plenty... Even friend Jonathan, who lived with him
  for weeks, did never see him to eat, never!"
- **No shadow, no reflection**: "He throws no shadow; he make in the
  mirror no reflect."
- **Transformations**: wolf (Whitby dog), bat (at windows), mist ("the
  distance he can make this mist is limited, and it can only be round
  himself"), "elemental dust" on moonlight rays (the sisters), slipping
  through hairbreadth spaces; "He can see in the dark."
- **LIMITS**: "He may not enter anywhere at the first, unless there be
  some one of the household who bid him to come; though afterwards he can
  come as he please." — "His power ceases, as does that of all evil
  things, at the coming of the day." — "If he be not at the place whither
  he is bound, he can only change himself at noon or at exact sunrise or
  sunset." — "He can only pass running water at the slack or the flood of
  the tide." — Garlic afflicts him; before sacred things "he take his
  place far off and silent with respect."
- **Kill/confine**: "The branch of wild rose on his coffin keep him that
  he move not from it; a sacred bullet fired into the coffin kill him so
  that he be true dead; and as for the stake through him, we know already
  of its peace; or the cut-off head that giveth rest."
- **Sacred-earth dependency**: "in soil barren of holy memories it cannot
  rest" — hence "we must, so to speak, sterilise the earth, so that no
  more he can seek safety in it. Thus in the end we may find him in his
  form of man between the hours of noon and sunset, and so engage with him
  when he is at his most weak."
- **History**: "He must, indeed, have been that Voivode Dracula who won
  his name against the Turk"; "They learned his secrets in the
  Scholomance... where the devil claims the tenth scholar as his due";
  "in one manuscript this very Dracula is spoken of as 'wampyr.'"
- **Stakes of failure**: "It is that we become as him... foul things of
  the night like him--without heart or conscience... To us for ever are
  the gates of heaven shut."

### 4.2 The Carfax raid (1 Oct, Harker's journal)

- Kit issued per hunter on the porch: silver crucifix ("Keep this near
  your heart"), "a wreath of withered garlic blossoms," revolver and
  knife, "small electric lamps, which you can fasten to your breast,"
  and "a portion of Sacred Wafer" in an envelope.
- SEWARD picks the lock: "Dr. Seward tried one or two skeleton keys, his
  mechanical dexterity as a surgeon standing him in good stead." VH
  crosses first: "In manus tuas, Domine!"
- The house: dust inches deep with hobnail footprints, "a great bunch of
  keys, with a time-yellowed label on each" on the hall table; VH carries
  a map of the house copied from the purchase correspondence; "a low,
  arched oaken door, ribbed with iron bands" = the chapel.
- The smell: "it seemed as though corruption had become itself corrupt."
- **The count: "There were only twenty-nine left out of the fifty!"**
- The rats: "a whole mass of phosphorescence, which twinkled like stars.
  The whole place was becoming alive with rats... multiplying in
  thousands." Godalming's "little silver whistle" summons three terriers
  who scatter them. Lesson: "these rats that would come to his call...
  run pell-mell from the so little dogs."
- Chess line: "It has given us opportunity to cry 'check' in some ways in
  this chess game, which we play for the stake of human souls."

### 4.3 The box trail (Ch. XX — the investigation chain)

50 total = 29 at Carfax + 6 at 197 Chicksand Street (Mile End) + 6 at
Jamaica Lane (Bermondsey) + 9 at No. 347 Piccadilly.
- Chain: Billington's invoice ("Fifty cases of common earth, to be used
  for experimental purposes") -> King's Cross -> Carter Paterson day-book
  -> Thomas Snelling (drunk, useless) -> Joseph Smollet (dog-eared pencil
  notebook: the two six-box addresses; tips paid in half-sovereigns) ->
  scrap of paper: "Sam Bloxam, Korkrans, 4, Poters Cort, Bartel Street,
  Walworth. Arsk for the depite." -> Bloxam at a Poplar cold-store: nine
  boxes to a Piccadilly house, and the payload detail — the old man
  loaded the boxes himself: "he was the strongest chap I ever struck...
  one that thin you would think he couldn't throw a shadder"; "'e took up
  'is end o' the boxes like they was pounds of tea."
- Finding the house: high stone front with a bow, high steps, near a big
  white church; dusty windows, shutters, torn "For Sale" board; agents
  Mitchell, Sons & Candy stonewall until Lord Godalming's name is played.
  Buyer: "a foreign nobleman, Count de Ville, who effected the purchase
  himself paying the purchase money in notes 'over the counter.'"
- Bribery-with-drink/coin is the recurring interview mechanic.

### 4.4 Renfield's plea, invitation, and death

- The lucid plea (1 Oct, refused): "Send keepers with me with whips and
  chains; let them take me in a strait-waistcoat, manacled and
  leg-ironed... but let me go out of this. You don't know what you do by
  keeping me here... I am no lunatic in a mad fit, but a sane man
  fighting for his soul!" Parting: "You will, I trust, Dr. Seward, do me
  the justice to bear in mind, later on, that I did what I could to
  convince you to-night."
- **The invitation** (deathbed confession): Dracula whispers at his
  window: "Rats, rats, rats! Hundreds, thousands, millions of them, and
  every one a life; and dogs to eat them, and cats too. All lives! all
  red blood, with years of life in it" — "before I knew what I was doing,
  I found myself opening the sash and saying to Him: 'Come in, Lord and
  Master!' ... He slid into the room through the sash, though it was only
  open an inch wide." THIS is how Dracula gains entry to the asylum.
- Why Renfield fought: "When Mrs. Harker came in to see me this afternoon
  she wasn't the same; it was like tea after the teapot had been
  watered... it made me mad to know that He had been taking the life out
  of her." He grapples the mist; "He raised me up and flung me down" —
  back broken, skull fractured; VH trephines to buy his confession:
  "His words may be worth many lives... It may be there is a soul at
  stake!"

### 4.5 The attack on Mina (Ch. XXI — the violation scene)

- The tableau: "On the bed beside the window lay Jonathan Harker, his
  face flushed and breathing heavily as though in a stupor... By her side
  stood a tall, thin man, clad in black... his right hand gripped her by
  the back of the neck, forcing her face down on his bosom... The
  attitude of the two had a terrible resemblance to a child forcing a
  kitten's nose into a saucer of milk to compel it to drink."
- The rout: VH holds up "the envelope which contained the Sacred Wafer.
  The Count suddenly stopped, just as poor Lucy had done outside the
  tomb, and cowered back." He escapes as "a faint vapour... trailed under
  the door."
- Dracula's speeches to Mina: "Silence! If you make a sound I shall take
  him and dash his brains out before your very eyes." — "It is not the
  first time, or the second, that your veins have appeased my thirst!" —
  "You, their best beloved one, are now to me, flesh of my flesh; blood
  of my blood; kin of my kin; my bountiful wine-press for a while; and
  shall be later on my companion and my helper... When my brain says
  'Come!' to you, you shall cross land or sea to do my bidding."
- The forced drinking: "with his long sharp nails opened a vein in his
  breast... seized my neck and pressed my mouth to the wound, so that I
  must either suffocate or swallow."
- Mina: "Unclean, unclean! I must touch him or kiss him no more."
- Collateral: Dracula burns the manuscript and the phonograph cylinders —
  "Thank God there is the other copy in the safe!"
- Enabler chain (fair-play tragedy): the men exclude Mina "for her own
  good"; she takes a sleeping draught ("I may have been foolish in thus
  depriving myself of the power of waking. I might want it."); Renfield's
  invitation lets the Count in.

## PART 5: THE BRAND, THE SANCTIFICATION, THE CHASE, THE KILL (Ch. XXII-XXVII)

### 5.1 The brand

- Van Helsing touches the Wafer to Mina's forehead — "it had seared it--had
  burned into the flesh as though it had been a piece of white-hot metal."
  Mina: "Unclean! Unclean! Even the Almighty shuns my polluted flesh! I
  must bear this mark of shame upon my forehead until the Judgment Day."
- VH's prophecy: "that red scar, the sign of God's knowledge of what has
  been, shall pass away, and leave your forehead as pure as the heart we
  know."
- The scar is a live status indicator all through the chase: it burns when
  news of the Count arrives; it bars Mina from carrying the Host ("the
  scar on my forehead forbids that"); peasants cross themselves and make
  the two-finger sign at it; it vanishes at the moment of the kill.

### 5.2 Sanctifying the boxes

- Method: open lid with screwdriver and wrench, "Taking from his box a
  piece of the Sacred Wafer he laid it reverently on the earth, and then
  shutting down the lid began to screw it home." Rationale: "We must
  sterilise this earth... He has chosen this earth because it has been
  holy. Thus we defeat him with his own weapon, for we make it more holy
  still."
- Box arithmetic (final, verified): 29 Carfax + 8 of 9 at Piccadilly + 6
  Bermondsey + 6 Mile End = 49 sterilized; **1 box escapes** on the
  Czarina Catherine — the coffin of the endgame. "There is but one more
  earth-box, and we must try to find it."

### 5.3 The Piccadilly raid (3 Oct)

- Entry by locksmith ruse: Godalming's title makes it respectable ("My
  title will make it all right with the locksmith, and with any policeman
  that may come along"); the others watch from Green Park; new key handed
  over for a tip; "not a soul took the slightest notice."
- Found inside: eight earth-boxes; on the great table title deeds for all
  the houses, "a clothes brush, brush and comb, and a jug and basin--the
  latter containing dirty water which was reddened as if with blood.
  Last of all was a little heap of keys of all sorts and sizes."
- Dracula bursts in: "with a single bound he leaped into the room...
  something so panther-like in the movement." Harker's Kukri slash: "the
  point just cut the cloth of his coat, making a wide gap whence a bundle
  of bank-notes and a stream of gold fell out." He grabs a handful of
  money and crashes through the window; from the stable door, the taunt:
  "You think to baffle me, you--with your pale faces all in a row, like
  sheep in a butcher's. You shall be sorry yet, each one of you!... Your
  girls that you all love are mine already; and through them you and
  others shall yet be mine--my creatures, to do my bidding and to be my
  jackals when I want to feed. Bah!"
- VH's read: "Notwithstanding his brave words, he fears us; he fear time,
  he fear want! For if not, why he hurry so?... Why take that money?"

### 5.4 The chase

- Mina's idea: "He must hypnotise me before the dawn, and then I shall be
  able to speak." Trance reports (only at sunrise/sunset): "The lapping
  of water... the creaking of a chain, and the loud tinkle as the check
  of the capstan falls into the rachet." Conclusion: "He meant escape.
  Hear me, ESCAPE!"
- The ship: the Czarina Catherine, Doolittle's Wharf, bound for Varna;
  box consigned to "Ristics" at Varna. Sea needs 3 weeks; overland 3
  days. Hunters take the Orient Express, wait at Varna (Hotel Odessus).
- The double-cross: Dracula reads Mina in trance and routes past Varna;
  telegram 28 Oct: "Czarina Catherine reported entering Galatz at one
  o'clock to-day." Mina, instantly: "When does the next train start for
  Galatz?... I am the train fiend."
- Galatz forensics: Captain Donelson (Scots: "as though the Deil himself
  were blawin' on yer sail for his ain purpose"); box claimed before
  sunrise by Immanuel Hildesheim on orders of "Mr. de Ville of London,"
  passed to Petrof Skinsky "who dealt with the Slovaks who traded down
  the river"; Skinsky found dead in St. Peter's churchyard, "the throat
  had been torn open as if by some wild animal."
- Mina's memorandum (process of exclusion): he must go by water, in an
  open boat poled upstream — "the Sereth is, at Fundu, joined by the
  Bistritza which runs up round the Borgo Pass." VH: "Our dear Madam Mina
  is once more our teacher."
- The three-way split: Godalming + Jonathan by steam launch up the
  Sereth/Bistritza; Seward + Morris on horseback along the bank with
  Winchesters ("I propose that we add Winchesters to our armament");
  Van Helsing + Mina by train to Veresti, then carriage 70+ miles over
  the Borgo Pass to the castle itself: "I will take Madam Mina right into
  the heart of the enemy's country."
- Mina is changing: sleeps by day, refuses food, "more healthy and more
  redder"; hypnosis fades; the men eventually cannot tell her plans (the
  Count can read her) — but VH: "That terrible baptism of blood which he
  give you makes you free to go to him in spirit."

### 5.5 The castle finale (Van Helsing's Memorandum, 4-5 Nov)

- The holy circle: "I drew a ring so big for her comfort, round where
  Madam Mina sat; and over the ring I passed some of the wafer, and I
  broke it fine so that all was well guarded." Test: Mina rises, steps,
  stops "as one stricken" — "I cannot!" — "I rejoiced, for I knew that
  what she could not, none of those that we dreaded could."
- The sisters come with the snow and mist: "the same three women that
  Jonathan saw in the room... Come, sister. Come to us. Come! Come!"
  Mina, laughing "low and unreal": "Fear for me! Why fear for me? None
  safer in all the world from them than I am." At dawn they melt toward
  the castle. The horses are all dead.
- In the castle: VH breaks the doors off their hinges with the Veresti
  blacksmith hammer "lest some ill-intent or ill-chance should close
  them"; uses Jonathan's diary as his map. The fair sister nearly takes
  him: "I was moved to a yearning for delay which seemed to paralyse my
  faculties and to clog my very soul" — Mina's far-off wail saves him.
- "There was one great tomb more lordly than all the rest; huge it was,
  and nobly proportioned. On it was but one word / DRACULA." He lays the
  Wafer in it, "and so banished him from it, Un-Dead, for ever."
- The staking of the three: "it was butcher work... the horrid screeching
  as the stake drove home; the plunging of writhing form, and lips of
  bloody foam" — then each body "began to melt away and crumble in to
  its native dust."

### 5.6 The kill (6 Nov, sunset, Mina's journal)

- The leiter-wagon "swept from side to side, like a dog's tail wagging...
  On the cart was a great square chest." VH: "They are racing for the
  sunset. We may be too late. God's will be done!" Wolves gather.
- Jonathan leaps on the cart and flings the box to the ground; Morris
  fights through the gypsy knives, "clutching at his side... the blood
  was spurting through his fingers"; the lid pried back, "the nails drew
  with a quick screeching sound."
- The Count: "deathly pale, just like a waxen image, and the red eyes
  glared with the horrible vindictive look... the eyes saw the sinking
  sun, and the look of hate in them turned to triumph."
- "But, on the instant, came the sweep and flash of Jonathan's great
  knife. I shrieked as I saw it shear through the throat; whilst at the
  same moment Mr. Morris's bowie knife plunged into the heart. It was
  like a miracle; but before our very eyes, and almost in the drawing of
  a breath, the whole body crumbled into dust and passed from our sight."
- "Even in that moment of final dissolution, there was in the face a look
  of peace." No stake needed at the end: two mortal knives, one second
  before sunset.
- Quincey dies pointing at Mina: "Now God be thanked that all has not
  been in vain! See! the snow is not more stainless than her forehead!
  The curse has passed away!" — "with a smile and in silence, he died, a
  gallant gentleman."
- The Note, seven years after: the Harkers' boy is called Quincey, born
  on the anniversary; "We want no proofs; we ask none to believe us!"

## PART 6: EPISTOLARY STRUCTURE + MASTER TIMELINE (compressed)

Narrating documents by phase:
- Ch. I-IV: Jonathan Harker's journal (castle) — 3 May-30 June.
- Ch. V-IX: Mina/Lucy letters, Mina's Whitby journal, the Dailygraph
  cutting + Demeter log, Seward's phonograph diary (Renfield), Van
  Helsing correspondence — May-6 Sept.
- Ch. X-XVI: Seward's diary spine + Lucy's diary/memorandum, telegrams,
  newspaper cuttings — 7 Sept-29 Sept (Lucy dies 20 Sept, staked 29th).
- Ch. XVII-XXI: Seward + Harker + Mina journals interleaved (the hunt) —
  29 Sept-3 Oct (Mina attacked the night of 2-3 Oct).
- Ch. XXII-XXVII: all hands + Van Helsing's Memorandum for the finale —
  3 Oct-6 Nov, plus the Note seven years later.

Master beat list: Harker at the castle (May-June) -> escapes via the wall,
brain fever -> Demeter voyage (July) -> storm lands Dracula at Whitby
(8 Aug) -> Lucy's decline (Aug-Sept) -> Lucy dies (20 Sept), staked
(29 Sept) -> hunters unite, Carfax raid, box hunt (30 Sept-2 Oct) ->
Renfield's invitation, Mina attacked (3 Oct) -> brand; 49 boxes
sanctified; Piccadilly confrontation (3 Oct) -> Dracula flees by sea
(4-5 Oct) -> Varna stakeout fails; Galatz (28-30 Oct) -> three-way chase
up the rivers -> brides staked, castle sealed (5 Nov) -> sunset kill at
the castle road; Quincey dies; scar vanishes (6 Nov).
